List of recommendations
Recommendation 1
2.54 The Committee recommends that the Department of Defence implements
measures to safeguard the privacy of Australian Defence Force members,
veterans and their families given the information collection, use and
disclosure measures in the Bill.
Recommendation 2
2.61 The Committee recommends that the Bill be amended to ensure that
recommendation 22 of the Royal Commission into Defence and Veteran
Suicide is implemented in full. Specifically, the Committee recommends that
the Bill be amended to make clear that a conviction of any sexual offence
(including stalking and intimate image abuse), not just serious violent and
sexual offences, regardless of the sentence imposed, prevent a person from
being accepted for service under proposed section 22A and require the end of
their service under proposed section 27A.
Recommendation 3
2.63 The Committee recommends that the Defence and Veterans’ Service
Commission includes information in its annual report about the number of
own motion final inquiry reports that were not tabled in Parliament within
the reporting period.
Recommendation 4
2.65 The Committee recommends that the Bill be amended to require that the
Surgeon General of the Australian Defence Force be a registered medical
practitioner with specialist qualifications.
Recommendation 5
2.68 The Committee recommends that the Defence and Veterans’ Service
Commission conduct a review into the effectiveness of the measures
implemented by the Bill within 24 months of the commencement of the Bill
and table its findings in Parliament.
Recommendation 6
2.69 The Committee recommends that subject to the above recommendations the
Senate pass the Bill.

Chapter 1
Introduction
Referral
1.1 On 25 June 2026, the Defence Legislation Amendment (RCDVS Implementation
and Related Measures No. 2) Bill 2026 (the Bill) was referred to the
Senate Foreign Affairs, Defence and Trade Legislation Committee
(the Committee) for inquiry and report by 24 August 2026.1
1.2 On 2 July 2026, the Senate agreed to extend the reporting date to
4 September 2026.2
Background
1.3 The Australian Government established the Royal Commission into Defence
and Veteran Suicide (RCDVS) in April 2021 following successful parliamentary
motions supporting a royal commission into suicide among serving and former
Australian Defence Force (ADF) personnel.3
1.4 The final report of the RCDVS made 122 recommendations, the majority of
which were accepted by the Australian Government.
1.5 Earlier in 2026, the Committee reported on the Defence Force Discipline
Amendment (RCDVS Implementation and Related Measures No. 1) Bill 2026.
That bill sought to implement recommendations from the RCDVS in relation to
services offences, the functions of the Director of Military Prosecutions and
other related matters.4
1.6 The Bill under consideration seeks to implement the RCDVS recommendations
relating to information sharing to support the health and wellbeing of current
and former ADF personnel, as well as ensuring individuals convicted of serious
violent and sexual offences cannot serve in the ADF.5

Purpose of the Bill
1.7 The Bill seeks to implement reforms to better enable the ADF, the
Department of Defence (Defence), the Department of Veterans’ Affairs (DVA)
and other commonwealth agencies to improve the health and wellbeing of
ADF personnel, veterans, and their families.6
1.8 It would do so by amending the Defence Act 1903 (Defence Act) to include a
dedicated part relating to health and wellbeing support for ADF members,
veterans and their families. The Bill would also amend the
Military Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 2004 (MRCA) to expand the
statutory functions of the Military Rehabilitation and Compensation
Commission (MRC Commission) to include supporting health and wellbeing
outcomes of ADF members, veterans and their families.7
1.9 The Bill seeks to implement a policy of mandatory discharge from the ADF for
members convicted of sexual and other serious offences which result in
punishment or imprisonment, as well as preventing those who have previously
been sentenced for a sexual offence from joining the ADF.8
1.10 The Bill would also seek to clarify the attendance obligations of persons
summoned by the Defence and Veterans’ Service Commission.
Key provisions
1.11 The Bill contains five schedules.
Schedule 1—Information sharing to improve wellbeing, health and safety
outcomes
1.12 Schedule 1 contains two parts and seeks to improve the timeliness and reporting
of information sharing between Defence and DVA while also improving
transparency.
Part 1—Amendments
1.13 The proposed amendments to the Defence Act seek to insert a new part,
‘Part VIAB—Health and wellbeing support for Defence members, veterans and
their families’. This new part would authorise the collection and use of
information about ADF members, veterans and their families for the following
two broad purposes:
 to facilitate the research, data analysis and evaluation relating to wellbeing,
health or safety; and

1.14 The collection, use and disclosure of this information would be limited to

relating to the disclosure of information if they are satisfied that the disclosure

1.15 The Minister may also, via legislative instrument, set out guidelines for the

relating to any specific projects. Before making these guidelines, the Minister
must seek advice from the Information Commissioner, and if the Minister is not
also the Minister for Veterans’ Affairs, the guidelines must be jointly made with
the Minister for Veterans’ Affairs.11
1.16 Schedule 1 would also amend the MRCA to enhance the statutory functions of
the MRC Commission to support ADF members transitioning out of service.
The explanatory memorandum (EM) explained that the Bill would establish an
information sharing framework and arrangements similar to the information
sharing provisions in the Defence Act, to better support the wellbeing, health
and safety of current and former ADF members and their families.12
1.17 The amendments contain provisions relating to the mechanisms for the
disclosure of information from Defence to the MRC Commission for the
purposes of performing a duty or function of the MRC Commission.13
Part 2—Application
1.18 Part 2 of Schedule 1 sets out that the amendments to the Defence Act and
the MRCA that apply to the collection, use or disclosure of the information
would apply even if the information was collected before the commencement of
the Bill. The EM outlines this as being necessary to ensure the continuity and
effectiveness of information sharing arrangements to avoid any disruption to
clinical care, wellbeing support, claims administration and other policy
activities.14

Schedule 2—Defence health services
1.19 Schedule 2 would amend the Defence Act to establish a clear framework for the
delivery of health services in the ADF, clarifying the roles in the health system,
as well as providing the ability to collect health information for monitoring and
quality assurance purposes.
Part 1 – Amendments
1.20 The Bill would seek to insert a ‘Division 3 —Defence Health Services’ into the
proposed section VIAB of the Defence Act, clarifying the role of defence health
services, the collection, usage and disclosure of the health information of
ADF members and arrangements for quality assurance and improvement
activities.15
1.21 Defence health services are defined as ‘health services provided, or arranged for,
by the Commonwealth to the groups listed in subsection 70L(2)’. These groups
include:
 ADF members (both permanent and reserve members);
 members of visiting forces;
 people undergoing assessment for fitness for service as an ADF member;
 people who are not ADF members, but who are involved in an ADF
operation; and
 people prescribed in regulation who are involved with or have a significant
connection to the ADF.16
Division 3 — Defence health system
1.22 Division 3 contains four subdivisions.
1.23 Subdivision A seeks to clarify the differences between civilian health services
and Defence health services by outlining that providing Defence health services
includes:
 keeping ADF members fit for the performance of their duties;
 supporting the lifetime wellbeing, health and safety of ADF members; and
 providing health services to other individuals for the purpose of supporting
the operation of the ADF.17
1.24 The EM explains that the intent of this is to ensure that the purposes of the
Defence health system are not just limited to ensuring operational fitness of
ADF members, but to support long-term wellbeing, while also setting a

Schedule 3—Other amendments to support ADF families
1.31 Schedule 3 seeks to amend the Defence Act to ensure that Defence can
adequately support former partners of ADF members as well as supporting
direct engagement between Defence and ADF family members.25
1.32 The Bill would amend section 58A of the Defence Act to insert a definition of a
‘former partner’ of an ADF member, described as:
 A member of the household of the member; and
 The member’s spouse or de facto partner (within the meaning of the
Acts Interpretation Act 1901).26
1.33 This definition is intended allow the Minister to provide the payment of
allowances or pecuniary benefits in limited circumstances as outlined in the Bill.
These circumstances are:
 the former partner is impacted by family and domestic violence
(within the meaning of the Fair Work Act 2009); and
 the payment or benefit is provided for the purposes of assisting the former
partner to safely separate from the member.27
1.34 These benefits would be limited to a 12-month period following separation from
the ADF member. The EM clarified that the purpose of this time limit is to
support an individual during their separation from an ADF member, rather than
providing ongoing support to a former partner.28
1.35 Schedule 3 would insert ‘Division 4 — Information sharing to support
ADF families’. This would establish authorisation for the collection, use and
disclosure of information of personal information about ADF family members
and former partners for limited purposes. These purposes would include:
 informing the ADF member or the ADF member’s family of the benefits
provide by the Commonwealth to support ADF family members or former
partners of ADF members; and
 providing the ADF member or the ADF member’s family a benefit provided
by the Commonwealth to support ADF family members or former partners
of ADF members.29

Schedule 4—Requirements to serve in the Defence Force
1.38 Schedule 4 seeks to amend the Defence Act to set out minimum standards for
serving in the ADF, including consolidating some existing provisions.
Schedule 4 would support the implementation of RCDVS Recommendation 22,
which in full states:
Defence should adopt a policy of mandatory discharge for
Australian Defence Force members convicted of sexual and related offences
(including stalking and intimate image abuse) in the military and civilian
criminal justice systems, subject to further legal advice on the legislative
barriers, if any.32
1.39 Schedule 4 is made up of three parts. Part 1 sets out proposed amendments to
the Defence Act and the MRCA, Part 2 sets out application provisions for the
amendments in Part 1 and Part 3 contains several contingent amendments,
which will only commence if the Defence Force Discipline Amendment (RCDVS
Implementation and Related Measures No. 1) Act 2026 has also commenced.33
Part 1—Amendments
1.40 The amendments in Part 1 seek to insert new section 22A into the Defence Act,
to restrict certain persons from joining the ADF. This would be a statutory
requirement that only ‘fit and proper persons may be accepted for service in the
ADF’.34

accepted for service:

Part 3—Contingency amendments
1.47 Part 3 of Schedule 4 would amend the Defence Act to insert a definition of

Schedule 5—Other amendments
1.48 Schedule 5 seeks to amend the Defence and Veterans’ Service Commissioner Act
2026 (DVSC Act) to give the option for final own-motion inquiry reports to be
tabled in Parliament at the request of the Defence and Veterans’ Service
Commissioner (DVSC). Currently, the DVSC Act requires any final inquiry
reports to be tabled in Parliament once they are received by the Minister.
This change would give the DVSC flexibility to provide reports to the Minister
for information or as a courtesy without triggering tabling requirements.
This amendment reverts the requirement that previously existed under the
Defence Act, which was inadvertently changed when the DVSC Act was
created.40
1.49 Schedule 5 also seeks to amend the DVSC Act to clarify attendance obligations
when a person is issued with a summons by the DVSC, requiring the person
issued with the summons to attend from day to day until excused by the DVSC.
The amendment would also expand the penalties of failing to appear when
summoned by the DVSC.41

Legislative scrutiny
1.53 The Senate Standing Committee for the Scrutiny of Bills (Scrutiny Committee)
report Scrutiny Digest 9 of 2026 raised the following concerns:
 Broad delegation of administrative powers: The CDF’s power to delegate
functions relating to the end of service of ADF members as set out in
Schedule 4 is considered too wide ranging, with non-SES members of the
APS being able to have these powers delegated to them, without adequate
justification being provided in the EM.45
 Significant matters in delegated legislation: The committee was concerned
that the provisions in Schedule 4 relating to the discretionary power to end
the service of an ADF member considered not to be a ‘fit and proper person’
are ill-defined and wide ranging. While the EM states that regulations to
provide for matters that may or must be taken into account when exercising
this power would include guidance on this matter, the Scrutiny Committee
was of the view that there was not sufficient justification in the EM for the
use of delegated legislation.46
1.54 As of writing the Minister had not yet responded to the concerns raised by the
Scrutiny Committee.

Chapter 2
Views on the Bill
2.1 This chapter examines submitter views on the Defence Legislation Amendment
(RCDVS Implementation and Related Measures No. 2) Bill 2026 (the Bill).
The chapter looks at:
 inquiry participants’ broad support for the Bill;
 suggested measures to strengthen aspects of the proposed legislation; and
 oversight of the implementation of the amendments.
2.2 Chapter 2 will also set out the Committee’s views and recommendations.
Support for the Bill
2.3 Evidence presented to the inquiry indicated there is wide-ranging support for
the objectives of the Bill. Submitters and witnesses broadly considered the
amendments proposed by the Bill to be a necessary step towards improving
health and wellbeing outcomes for Australian Defence Force (ADF) members,
veterans and their families.
2.4 Inquiry participants emphasised that the Bill is a direct response to
recommendations of the Royal Commission into Defence and Veteran Suicide
(RCDVS).
2.5 The Defence and Veterans’ Service Commission (DVSC) confirmed its support
for the proposed amendments included in the Bill. The DVSC explained that it
supports ‘any improvements to the defence ecosystem which will positively
impact the wellbeing of our veterans and/ or decrease the likelihood of suicide’.1
2.6 The Inspector-General of the Australian Defence Force (IGADF) concluded that
the proposed measures would ‘strengthen the wellbeing, health and safety
framework for ADF members, veterans and their families’.2
2.7 The Defence Force Ombudsman (DFO) similarly expressed support for
legislative reform that would implement RCDVS recommendations relating to
the wellbeing of ADF members, veterans and their families, and would address
issues of sexual violence in the ADF.3

Committee view
2.50 The Committee acknowledges that the establishment of the RCDVS was a long
and challenging process and appreciates the contributions of serving
ADF members, veterans and families to the work of the RCDVS. The Committee
welcomes the Bill as a further step in implementing the recommendations of the
RCDVS by improving the health and wellbeing outcomes of ADF members,
veterans, and their families.
2.51 Evidence received by the Committee reflected broad support for the Bill and the
meaningful reforms it seeks to implement, specifically noting the importance of
measures relating to improved data collection and sharing, legislating Defence
health standards for the first time and taking steps to more adequately address
the impact of sexual offences that occur in the ADF.
2.52 The Committee appreciates community input on the proposed legislation, and
notes community concerns regarding the risk of privacy breaches, transparency
in data sharing arrangements and the importance of individuals being able to
ensure that their records are accurate.
2.53 The Committee recognises the importance of trust in the relationship ADF
members, veterans and their families have with the legislative framework
governing information sharing and agrees that clear safeguards must be in place
to protect the personal information of ADF members, veterans and their
families. The Committee therefore shares the view that robust governance must
be established to protect privacy. The Committee is confident that, supported
by the additional measures recommended by this report, the Bill will enable
appropriate oversight of the proposed framework and maintain trust among
ADF members, veterans and their families.
Recommendation 1
2.54 The Committee recommends that the Department of Defence implements
measures to safeguard the privacy of Australian Defence Force members,

2.55 The Committee notes that the intent of schedule 4 of the Bill is to support the
implementation of recommendation 22 of the RCDVS, which calls for Defence
to ‘adopt a policy of mandatory discharge for [ADF] members convicted of
sexual and related offences (including stalking and intimate image abuse)’.44
2.56 The Committee is of the view that the Bill, as drafted, is too narrow in scope to
fully implement recommendation 22 of the RCDVS.
2.57 In particular, the requirement that an ADF member be both convicted and
receive a sentence of imprisonment (or a suspended sentence of imprisonment)
before mandatory discharge from the ADF applies, sets a different threshold
than recommended by the Royal Commission.
2.58 The higher threshold of a sentence of imprisonment, rather than just conviction,
would fail to exclude many convicted of sexual offences from serving in the
ADF, including those convicted of the crimes explicitly referred to by the
RCDVS – stalking and intimate image abuse – a majority of whom are not
sentenced to imprisonment following conviction.
2.59 This higher threshold for mandatory exclusion from service would also
represent a less strict approach to sexual offences than the UK armed forces,
whose zero-tolerance approach the RCDVS pointed to as one the ADF should
adopt.
2.60 The Committee recognises the importance of the need to maintain discipline,
integrity, and public confidence in the ADF and its personnel by ensuring there
is a zero-tolerance approach established for sexual and related offences. In light
of the findings of the Royal Commission, the Committee does not consider it
appropriate that anyone convicted of a sexual offence by an Australian court
should be allowed to serve in uniform.
Recommendation 2
2.61 The Committee recommends that the Bill be amended to ensure that
recommendation 22 of the Royal Commission into Defence and Veteran
Suicide is implemented in full. Specifically, the Committee recommends that
the Bill be amended to make clear that a conviction of any sexual offence
(including stalking and intimate image abuse), not just serious violent and
sexual offences, regardless of the sentence imposed, prevent a person from
being accepted for service under proposed section 22A and require the end of
their service under proposed section 27A.

Coalition additional comments
1.1 The Coalition recognises the importance of implementing the recommendations
of the Royal Commission into Defence and Veteran Suicide and supports
measures that strengthen the health and wellbeing of serving Australian
Defence Force members, veterans and their families. The Coalition also supports
improvements to the collection and use of data to inform research, policy
development and analysis, together with reforms that enhance the effectiveness,
transparency and accountability of the Defence and Veterans' Service
Commission.
1.2 The Coalition notes ongoing concerns raised by serving Australian Defence
Force members, veterans and ex-service organisations regarding the expanded
information-sharing provisions contained within the Bill. While supporting the
intent of measures designed to improve coordination and service delivery, the
Coalition considers that further consideration should be given to strengthening
privacy safeguards and individual protections. The Coalition encourages the
Government to continue engaging with veterans and to address these concerns
during the implementation of the proposed reforms. In particular, evidence
provided to the inquiry highlighted concerns that information relating to
entitlements, financial circumstances or other personal matters could be
disclosed in a manner that may have unintended consequences for family law
proceedings or other private legal matters.
1.3 The Coalition acknowledges the evidence of RSL Australia that reforms of this
nature should be accompanied by clear information for Australian Defence
Force members and veterans outlining their privacy rights, the circumstances in
which information may be shared, and the safeguards that apply to the handling
of personal information. The Coalition also considers that accessible and
transparent complaints mechanisms should be established to enable individuals
to raise concerns about potential breaches of privacy and to ensure confidence
in the operation of the proposed framework.

their contribution and service to Australia.
