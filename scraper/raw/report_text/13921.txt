Chapter 1
Introduction and background
Referral of the Bill
1.1 On 28 August 2025, the Defence Amendment (Defence Honours and Awards
Appeal Tribunal) Bill 2025 (the Bill) was introduced into the House of
Representatives by the Minister for Veterans' Affairs and the Minister for
Defence Personnel, the Hon Matt Keogh MP.1
1.2 On 4 September 2025, pursuant to the Senate Selection of Bills Committee report,
the provisions of the Bill were referred to the Senate Foreign Affairs, Defence
and Trade Legislation Committee (the Committee) for inquiry and report by
30 October 2025.2 On 28 October 2025, the Senate agreed to extend the reporting
date to the 21 November 2025.3
Structure of the report
1.3 This report contains two chapters:
 Chapter 1 – covers the purpose and key provisions of the Bill, as well as
providing background information. Chapter 1 also explores the Bills
compatibility with human rights.
 Chapter 2 – canvases the key issues raised by inquiry participants.
Chapter 2 also sets out the Committee's view and recommendation.
Purpose of the Bill
1.4 The purpose of the Bill is to amend the Defence Act 1903 (Defence Act) to allow
for changes to enabling regulations under Part 6 of Defence Regulations 2016 to
modify review arrangements by the Defence Honours and Awards Appeals
Tribunal (the Tribunal) for defence awards, defence honours and foreign
awards.4
1.5 In their second reading speech, the Minister for Veterans' Affairs and Minister
for Defence Personnel, the Hon Matt Keogh MP (the Minister), provided an
overview of the amendments:
… to assist the tribunal in their review function, this bill amends the
jurisdiction of the tribunal as set out in the Defence Act 1903 in a number of
important ways: by updating the time period for which the tribunal can

Background
1.8 The Australian honours and awards system provides tangible recognition for
acts of gallantry and bravery; distinguished, conspicuous, meritorious or
noteworthy service; and acknowledges individual, or group, commitment and
contribution of Australians. Defence honours and awards are nested within the
Australian honours and awards system.8

1.9 There are three distinct categories of defence honours and awards:
(a) Service awards recognise commitment and length of service in the ADF.
(b) Operational and campaign awards recognise individual service during
military campaigns or on operations.
(c) Honours and decorations recognise achievement, dedication, courage and
tenacity for individuals and unit recognition of groups of Australian
Defence Force (ADF) personnel.9
1.10 Defence administers the defence honours and awards system by taking
applications, assessing eligibility and either recommending the award or
providing a letter to the applicant explaining why the Defence honour or award
is being refused.10
The Defence Honours and Awards Appeals Tribunal
1.11 The Tribunal was established on an administrative basis in 2008, and in 2011 the
current iteration of the Tribunal was established through legislation as an
independent statutory body.11
1.12 Part VIIIC of the Defence Act establishes the Tribunal and confers on it two core
functions:
(d) to review Defence decisions against recommending an ADF member or
veteran for a Defence honour or a defence award; and
(e) when directed to do so by the Minister, to inquire into and report to the
Minister on matters related to Defence honours and awards.12
1.13 Under current legislation, anyone can make an application to Defence for a
defence honour or award in respect to any service (after 3 September 1939) by a
current or former serving member of the ADF. Consequently, any unsuccessful
applicant can then apply at any time to the Tribunal for a review of a decision
from Defence to not recommend a defence honour or award to an ADF
member.13
1.14 On completion of a review, the Tribunal can make the following
recommendations:
 if the applicant is seeking a defence honour, the Tribunal is required to
make a recommendation to the Minister to either affirm Defence’s decision,

Parliamentary review
1.15 The Senate Foreign Affairs, Defence and Trade References Committee
(References Committee) conducted an inquiry into the Defence honours and
awards system, which tabled its final report in June 2025.15
1.16 As part of its submission to the References Committee's inquiry, Defence
included a number of proposals to change the defence honours and awards
system. One of these proposals was the change of the reviewable period for
honours and awards to those within a period of 20 years from when the service
occurred or when the relevant operation ceased.16
1.17 The final report highlighted the Tribunal's opposition to Defence's proposed
legislative reforms, particularly around the proposed eligibility criteria for
applicants and time limitations.17
1.18 The References Committee made six recommendations including a
recommendation that Defence work alongside the Tribunal to undertake a
review of Part VIIIC of the Defence Act to improve the Tribunal's review and
inquiry function.18
1.19 The Australian Government provided a response to the recommendations and
agreed with four recommendations, agreed in principle to one recommendation
and noted one recommendation.19

Provisions of the Bill
1.20 There is one schedule to the Bill, which amends the Defence Act. The key
provisions of Schedule 1 are outlined in the sections below.
Updated definitions
Defence award
1.21 Item 1 of the Bill would repeal the current definition of defence award and
substitutes in a new definition that clarifies that a defence award means a length
of service award or an operational service award.20
1.22 Item 4 of the Bill would insert the following definitions:
 length of service award: an award that is specified in the regulations as a
length of service award, and includes a clasp, bar, accumulated service
device, ribbon or other insignia or decoration associated with the length of
service award; and
 operational service award: an award that is specified in the regulations as
an operational service award, and includes a clasp, bar, accumulated service
device, ribbon or other insignia or decoration associated with the
operational service award.21
1.23 The EM states that the purpose of item 4 is to enable the operation of a new
subsection, inserted by item 8 of the Bill, which provides additional rules for the
defence awards. Additionally, the EM clarified that a decision regarding clasps
and other insignia associated with a relevant defence award is a reviewable
decision.22
Defence honour
1.24 Item 2 of the Bill would repeal the current definition of defence honour and
substitute in a new definition which states that a defence honour means an
honour specified in the regulations as a defence honour, and includes a clasp,
bar, accumulated service device, ribbon or other insignia or decoration
associated with the honour referred to in the regulations.23
1.25 The EM explains that the purpose of item 2 is to clarify that a decision regarding
clasps and other insignia associated with a relevant defence honour is a
reviewable decision.24

Additional rules for defence honours and awards
1.28 Item 8 of the Bill would repeal subsection 110V(2) and add new subsections
(2) to (9). The consequence of this amendment is that a reviewable decision is no
longer a decision that is made, or relates to service rendered, after
3 September 1939.
1.29 New subsections 110V(2) to (9) provide additional rules for decisions regarding
defence honours, operational service awards, length of service awards, foreign
awards, and cancellation decisions.
Defence honour
1.30 Subsection 110V(2) provides that a decision is not a reviewable decision if:
(a) the decision is a refusal to recommend a person or group of persons for
a defence honour; and
(b) the defence honour relates to a particular operation that has an end date;
and
(c) the end date was more than 20 years (or such lesser period as is
prescribed by the regulations) before the day the application referred to
in paragraph 110V(1)(c) was made.27
1.31 In summary, for a refusal decision regarding a defence honour that relates to a
particular operation with an end date to be a reviewable decision, an application
must be made to Defence within 20 years of the operation ending.28
1.32 For operations that do not relate to a particular operation, or which don't have
an end date, new subsection 110V(3) would provide that, for a refusal decision
regarding a defence honour under these circumstances to be a reviewable
decision, the application needs to be made to Defence within 20 years of the
eligible service being rendered.29

Applications for review of refusal decisions
1.44 Under the current legislation, anyone can apply at any time to Defence for any
defence honour to be issued to any member of the Australian Defence Force
(ADF). If the applicant is unsuccessful, they can then apply at any time to the
Tribunal for review as long as the service was rendered after 3 September 1939.41
1.45 Proposed subsections 110VA(1) to (3) would insert specifications regarding who
could apply for reviews of reviewable decisions.
1.46 For applications related to a defence honour, subsection 110VA(1) specifies that
a person (the applicant) may apply for a review of a reviewable decision that
relates to a refusal to recommend a person (the affected person) if:
(a) the applicant made the application referred to in paragraph 110V(1)(c),
or is one of the persons who made such an application; and
(b) one or both of the following applies:
(i) the applicant is or was more senior in the chain of command of the
affected person;
(ii) the applicant is or was a member of the ADF who witnessed the
eligible service to which the decision relates; and
(c) the affected person, or an immediate family member of the affected
person, consents to the making of the application for review.42
1.47 For applications related to a defence award or foreign award, subsections
110VA(2) and (3) state that a person (the applicant) may apply for a review of a
reviewable decision that relates to a refusal to recommend a person (the affected
person) for a defence award or foreign award if:
(a) the applicant made the application referred to in paragraph 110V(1)(c),
or is one of the persons who made such an application; and
(b) one of the following applies:
(i) the applicant is the affected person,
(ii) the application is an immediate family member of the affected
person; or
(iii) if the affected person is deceased—the applicant is the executor,
administrator, trustee of the estate or other personal representative
of the affected person.43
1.48 For clarity, the proposed section 110VA provides definitions for 'immediate

Time limit for making an application for review
1.49 Proposed section 110VAA provides that an application for review of a
reviewable decision can only be made within six months after the day the
reviewable decision is made. Alternatively, if the Tribunal is satisfied on
reasonable grounds that exceptional circumstances exist as to why an
application for review cannot be made within six months, the Tribunal may
allow a longer period for review.45
Other provisions

other proposed amendments, including:
 Recommendations to the Minister – Item 10 of the Bill would repeal and
replace paragraph 110VB(1)(b) to provide that the Tribunal may make
recommendations to the Minister, but only as to whether the reviewable
decision is consistent with the eligibility criteria of the defence honour at the

Parliament.48
 Regulations – Item 17 of the Bill would replace section 110Z to clarify what
matters the regulations may provide for.49
 Application and saving provisions – Item 20 sets out when the
amendments made by the Bill would apply, as well as clarifies details
regarding annual reporting requirements.50

Chapter 2
Key issues and views
2.1 The establishment of a dedicated body to review and examine unsuccessful
defence honours and awards nominations is a unique feature of the Australian
system. The Defence Honours and Awards Appeals Tribunal (the Tribunal)
provides an additional level of institutional accountability and transparency,
ensuring that decisions are reasonably and fairly made, and systemic issues can
be appropriately examined.
2.2 Evidence to the inquiry showed strong support for and confidence in the work
of the Tribunal. Notably, inquiry participants valued the independent nature of
the Tribunal, its impartiality and its thorough approach when handling reviews
and inquiries.
2.3 According to the Department of Defence (Defence), a small number of issues
regarding the function and processes of the Tribunal have become apparent, and
the provisions of the Defence Amendment (Defence Honours and Awards
Appeals Tribunal) Bill 2025 (the Bill) would address these issues with the aim of
contemporising the Tribunal's operation and thereby ensuring the Tribunal
remains fit-for-purpose.1
2.4 However, inquiry participants highlighted several concerns regarding the
proposed amendments to the Tribunal's review functions encompassed within
the Bill. In particular, submitters and witnesses raised concerns with:
 the proposed limits to the timeframe within which decisions relating to a
defence honour and award could be reviewed by the Tribunal;
 the proposed limits to who could submit an application to the Tribunal
regarding a reviewable decision;
 the proposed limits to when an applicant could seek a review from the
Tribunal;
 the arrangements regarding the Tribunal's ability to review cancelled
awards;
 the reduction in the Tribunal's powers of recommendations; and
 the inclusion of new powers to make regulations.
2.5 This chapter will examine each of the abovementioned concerns, before
concluding with the Committee's views and recommendation.

Timeframe for reviewable decisions
2.6 Under current legislation, any person may apply to Defence for any defence
honour or award in respect to any service by a current or former Australian
Defence Force (ADF) member. On the other hand, the Tribunal's review function
is limited to unsuccessful decisions made by Defence where the service in
question was rendered on or after the commencement of the Second World War
on 3 September 1939.2
2.7 The Bill would change the timeframe within which decisions relating to defence
honours and awards may be reviewed by the Tribunal. Under the proposed
amendments, applications for review must be made within 20 years of the
relevant operation ending or service being rendered, or before the affected
person reaches 100 years of age in the case of length of service awards.3
Timeframe for defence honours, operational service awards and foreign awards
2.8 The Bill would amend the Defence Act 1903 (Defence Act) to restrict the
Tribunal's remit to a Defence decision to decline a defence honour, operational

Committee view
2.95 The Committee acknowledges the important role of the Tribunal in providing
independent review of decisions relating to defence honours, defence awards
and foreign awards. Since its establishment as a statutory body in 2011, the
Tribunal has played a key role in ensuring transparency, fairness, and
recognition for ADF members and veterans.
2.96 The Committee appreciates that the Bill’s objective is to modernise and clarify
the jurisdiction of the Tribunal by amending the Tribunal’s review
arrangements for defence awards, defence honours and foreign awards.
2.97 The Committee recognises that a common concern raised by most inquiry
participants relates to the proposed amendment to limit an applicant’s review
rights to 20 years from the end of an operation, or if the operation has no end
date, within 20 years of the service being rendered.
2.98 The Committee acknowledges the justification provided by Defence is to ensure
that decisions are made while reliable evidence and witnesses are still accessible.
2.99 The Committee observes that Australia is unique among Commonwealth
nations in providing for independent administrative review via a tribunal.
2.100 Furthermore, when reviewing similar jurisdictions such as the United Kingdom,
Canada and New Zealand, the Committee found these countries limited review
to internal process with a review period of between two and five years.
2.101 In relation to reviewing historical cases, the Tribunal stated that it routinely
reviews decisions where the service in question is more than 20 years old.
Furthermore, that it experiences little if any difficulty in obtaining relevant and
reliable evidence to allow it to make sound decisions in relation to conflicts
dating back to the Second World War.
2.102 The Committee also heard that for classified operations, records may not be
declassified within the proposed 20-year limit, which could unfairly impact
individuals seeking medallic recognition for such operations as all the evidence
may not be available to consider.
2.103 Ultimately, the Committee is persuaded by the evidence that further
consideration and consultation is required on whether this is a need to include
a 20-year time restriction for reviews to the Tribunal, and if so, extending the
timeframe.
2.104 Submitters and witnesses also raised concerns relating to the proposal to restrict
who may apply for a review, and in particular, restricting the range of potential
applicants to ‘immediate family’.
2.105 The Committee acknowledges that these changes would align legislation
governing the Tribunal with other Commonwealth legislation, including the
31
Family Law Act 1975 and would ensure that only those with a direct connection
to the ADF member or veteran may seek a review.
2.106 However, the Committee is mindful that the definition of immediate family may
be outdated, increase complexity within the system and arbitrarily restrict who
can seek a review by the Tribunal.
2.107 The Committee notes that the cases of Teddy Sheean VC and Private Richard
Norden VC have received significant public attention. The Committee
acknowledges that Defence would retain the ability to review applications for
historical defence honours and awards. Further to this, the Bill would retain the
provision that enables the Minister for Defence Personnel to direct the Tribunal
to undertake an inquiry.
2.108 Given the personal and symbolic importance of honours and awards to current
and former members of the ADF and their families, the Committee considers
that broader consultation could strengthen the Bill and improve public
confidence in the proposed reforms. In addition, the Committee considers it
integral that the outcome of the consultations is made public to allow the Senate
to properly reconsider the provisions contained in the Bill.
Recommendation 1
2.109 The Committee recommends that the Department of Defence undertakes
further consultations with the Defence Honours and Awards Appeals
Tribunal, ex-service organisations and other relevant stakeholders, regarding
subsection 110V(2) of the Bill relating to the 20-year limitation on reviewable
decisions and section 110VA of the Bill relating to the standing provisions.
Recommendation 2
2.110 The Committee recommends that the Senate defer its consideration of the Bill
until the Department of Defence concludes and makes public the outcome of
its consultations.

1.7 Not surprisingly, the Tribunal itself has opposed this reform. In its submission,
it warned that these changes would abolish the current right of ADF members,
veterans and their supporters to seek an independent merits review of eligibility
for gallantry, distinguished and conspicuous service honours.1 When the very
government body being restructured opposes the legislation, the government

1.8 The Bill also seeks to restrict who may apply for a review, limiting it to the
member, immediate family or, in some cases, a senior ADF officer or eyewitness.

unable to pursue an application themselves. Their families, advocates and
supporters have long carried that burden for them, ensuring that their stories of
service and sacrifice are not lost.
1.9 The Bill should never have seen the light of day. The government’s time and
resources would be far better spent addressing the genuine priorities identified
by the veteran community. This includes implementing recommendations from
the Royal Commission into Defence and Veteran Suicide, improving mental
health and wellbeing services, tackling military sexual violence and bullying,
clearing the unacceptable backlog of claims, aligning NDIS and DVA rates, and
enhance education and transition pathways for serving and former members.
1.10 Instead, we have a Bill that fixes nothing and undermines the principle of
fairness that lies at the heart of veterans’ recognition. By curtailing review,
limiting eligibility and placing artificial time limits on proper recognition, the
Bill betrays the very people that the defence honours and awards program aims
to recognise. For that reason, I have circulated a committee of the whole
amendment that seeks to omit the Bill in its entirety, except for its one
worthwhile element: the requirement for the Tribunal to prepare and report to
the Minister each financial year on its operations and require the Minister to
table that report in Parliament.

inquiry, oppose the Bill, which can only be seen as an extraordinary and
unanimous rejection from the community. The only submission of support came
from Defence. The message from veterans, families, advocates and ex-service

1.1 I thank the Senate Foreign Affairs, Defence and Trade Legislation Committee

1.2 This Bill should be withdrawn from the Senate.
1.3 No significant evidence has been provided to the Committee to justify this Bill
or demonstrate that it should take priority in the legislative agenda.
1.4 I am not satisfied that the Government has provided a meaningful case for
change or demonstrated that the Defence Honours and Awards Appeals

1.5 During the public hearing, officials from the Department of Defence (Defence)

time limit proposal in that inquiry, which now forms part of this Bill. To quote
