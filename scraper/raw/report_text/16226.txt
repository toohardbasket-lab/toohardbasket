First report of 2026
e-petitions
1.1 On 5 February 2026, on the motion of Senator Payman, the Senate referred the
following matter to the committee for inquiry and report by 11 May 2026:
The appropriateness of amending standing orders 69, 70 and 71 to allow for the
creation of a system for the initiation of e-petitions, similar to the system used
by the House of Representatives, with particular reference to:
(a) making it easier for Australians to petition their elected representatives in
the Senate;
(b) the rise in non-conforming petitions in recent years;
(c) the environmental impact of printing out the entirety of petitions for
presentation or tabling, which may contain thousands of signatures; and
(d) any other related matters.
Discussion
1.2 The committee considered the matter at a meeting on 4 March 2026.
1.3 The committee noted an answer to an estimates question on notice from Senator
Payman (attached) containing statistics on the recent use of the petitions process
in the Senate. The committee also noted that, under the existing standing orders,
senators may present petitions on which signatures have been collected
electronically: see Odgers’ Australian Senate Practice, 14th ed., p. 592. Finally,
the committee noted challenges to the House of Representatives system for
e-petitions being posed by new technologies, including artificial intelligence.
Recommendation
1.4 The committee resolved not to recommend the creation of a similar system for
the Senate at this time.
