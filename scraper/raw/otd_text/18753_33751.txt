Overview
The Australian Government notes the report by the Joint Standing Committee on Treaties (the
committee) into Council of Europe Convention on the Manipulation of Sports Competitions, and this
Government Response is in response to Recommendations 2 and 3 of the report, as these are the
recommendations related to the Macolin Convention.
Australia has a strong international reputation for sport integrity.1 This is underpinned by
sophisticated approaches to monitoring sport integrity by a number of sports organisations and
wagering service providers. However, sport integrity matters are beyond the control of any single
stakeholder. They are international and connected, forming a complex threat matrix exposing
vulnerabilities that require a robust and nationally coordinated response.
The Macolin Convention is an initiative of the Council of Europe with the purpose of galvanising and
harmonising a collective response to ‘combat the manipulation of sports competitions in order to
protect the integrity of sport and sports ethics in accordance with the principle of the autonomy of
sport’.2 Australia was one of seven non-Council of Europe States which took part in drafting the
Macolin Convention.
The Macolin Convention is the only multilateral treaty promoting cooperation and regulation to
prevent the manipulation of sports competitions. It requests public authorities of States Parties to
cooperate with sports organisations, betting operators and competition organisers to prevent,
detect and sanction the manipulation of sports competitions. It proposes a common legal framework
for efficient international cooperation to respond to this increasingly globalised threat.
Ratification of the Macolin Convention and establishment of national match-fixing criminal
legislation will further strengthen the global response to international sports integrity matters,
acknowledging the transnational nature of competition manipulation and related corruption in
sport, and recognising the global quantity of threats to the integrity of sports competitions held in
Australia.
The Macolin Convention resolves Australia’s present lack of a cohesive national capability to
confront and respond to domestic and international competition manipulation and sports betting-
related corruption. Australia becoming a party to the Macolin Convention provides a framework for
the Commonwealth to enact criminal legislation to prevent and punish corruption in sports and
designate a National Platform for information sharing on such activities, supported by the external
affairs power.3
Ratification will consolidate Australia’s efforts to engage and respond to integrity threats in a formal
framework designed to promote information sharing and coordination.

Australian Government response
The Australian Government’s response to Recommendations 2 and 3 of the Council of Europe
Convention on the Manipulation of Sports Competitions is set out in detail below.
Recommendation 2:
The Committee recommends that Australia seek to encourage the development and be an early
adopter of similar treaties beyond Europe aimed at combatting the manipulation of sports.
The Government notes this recommendation.
The Committee’s recommendation is noted and aligns with Australia’s strong commitment to
addressing competition manipulation at both domestic and international levels. At present, the
Council of Europe’s Macolin Convention remains the only international treaty specifically designed
to combat the manipulation of sports competitions, and there is considerable benefit in prioritising
broader global adoption of this existing framework rather than developing parallel instruments.
Universal or near-universal uptake of the Macolin Convention would better enable coordinated
international responses to match-fixing and related integrity threats. In this context, Australia’s well-
established National Platform demonstrates significant capability and provides a practical model for
cross-sector collaboration and information sharing.
Australia will continue to support international partners by sharing expertise, assisting with the
establishment and operationalisation of national platforms, and actively advocating for Macolin
Convention ratification as the most effective means of strengthening the global response to
competition manipulation.

Recommendation 3:
The Committee supports the Council of Europe Convention on the Manipulation of Sports
Competitions and recommends binding treaty action be taken.
The Government supports this recommendation.
The Macolin Convention addresses the global, often transnational, threat of competition
manipulation and illegal sports betting. The Convention is designed to protect the integrity of sports
through encouraging cooperation between public authorities, sport organisations and betting
operators.
Australia is committed to ratifying the Macolin Convention and the Government is actively taking
steps to ensure our compliance with the Convention. As foreshadowed by the Prime Minister in
April 2026, the Government introduced legislation4 to amend the Criminal Code 1995 (Cth)
to establish National Sport Integrity Offences to complement existing State, Territory and other
Commonwealth laws giving effect to Australia’s obligations under the Macolin Convention.
