Introduction
The Offshore Petroleum and Greenhouse Gas Storage Legislation Amendment (Safety and
Other Measures) Bill 2024 (Bill) was introduced into the House of Representatives on
15 February 2024.
On 29 February 2024, the Senate referred the provisions of the Bill to the Senate Economics
Legislation Committee (Committee) for inquiry and report by 22 March 2024.
The committee recommended that the Bill be passed.
The Bill passed the Senate on 16 May 2024 with Government amendments that removed Part 2
of Schedule 2 of the Bill and clarified that contractors are included in the protection against
discrimination provisions. The Bill passed the House of Representatives on 28 May 2024.
The Bill received Royal Assent on 11 June 2024 and became the Offshore Petroleum and
Greenhouse Gas Storage Legislation Amendment (Safety and Other Measures) Act 2024. The
last provisions of the Act commenced on 12 June 2025.
Recommendations
In the report, the Committee recommended that the Bill be passed. The three additional
dissenting reports from the Australian Greens, Senator David Pocock and Senator Lidia Thorpe
each recommended that Part 2 of Schedule 2 of the Bill be removed or the Bill be opposed.
Recommendations that the Bill be passed/not passed are not addressed in this Government
response.
There are two additional recommendations from the Australian Greens and ten additional
recommendations from Senator Lidia Thorpe. The Government’s response to each dissenting
recommendation is provided below.
Australian Greens’ dissenting report - March 2024
Recommendation 2: That the Offshore Petroleum and Greenhouse Gas Storage legislative
framework be amended to ensure that:
 First Nations people, Traditional Owners and Knowledge Holders are included in the
definition of ‘relevant people’;
 First Nations people, Traditional Owners and Knowledge Holders have a veto power
over any developments in their sea country; and
 During any consultations for Offshore Project Proposals and/or Environment Plans,
proponents are required to identify areas of underwater cultural heritage, both
tangible and intangible, how the activity may impact it and how these potential
impacts can be avoided or mitigated.
The Government notes this recommendation.
In the 2023-24 Budget, the Government committed $12 million over three years to a review of
the environmental management regime for offshore petroleum and greenhouse gas activities to
ensure it is fit for purpose for a decarbonising economy. The review will also consider
opportunities to provide regulatory and administrative certainty for carbon capture and storage
activities.
2
Consultation with affected stakeholders and communities is an important aspect of Australia’s
offshore regulatory regime for petroleum and greenhouse gas storage activities. The Offshore
Environment Management Review includes a focus on clarifying existing consultation
requirements for offshore activities, including with First Nations people and communities, to
ensure consultation is targeted and effective.
The Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water is considering an
approach to First Nations engagement in the context of reforms to Commonwealth
environmental and cultural heritage protection laws that is inclusive and respectful of cultural
authority.
The Native Title Act 1993 (Cth) (NTA) provides procedural requirements for ‘future acts’
which have the potential to impact native title rights and interests, including in offshore places
(per s 24NA(8) of the NTA). While this does not provide a power of veto to native title holders
or claimants, it provides procedural rights at a minimum. The Australian Law Reform
Commission (ALRC) completed its review of the NTA’s future acts regime and provided its
Final Report to the Attorney-General in March 2026. The report was tabled in Parliament on
24 June 2026.

Recommendation 7: Australia cannot pursue any more new coal and gas projects if we are
to meet our emissions reduction obligations under the Paris Agreement.
The Government notes this recommendation.
This recommendation is not within the scope of the Bill.
Australia has national emissions reduction targets of 43% by 2030 on 2005 levels, 62-70% by
2035, and Net Zero by 2050. These targets apply to all parts of the economy, and all
greenhouse gases. Australia’s Net Zero Plan articulates how Australia will transition to a net
zero economy, consistent with our international and domestic commitments. The Net Zero Plan
is underpinned by six sectoral plans covering all major sectors of the economy, including the
4
resources sector. The resources sector plan will identify opportunities to reduce emissions from
all activities in scope, including coal and gas projects.
The Safeguard Mechanism plays a key role in incentivising long-term investment in emissions
reduction for the country’s largest industrial facilities, including coal mines and gas projects.
All covered facilities are required to steadily reduce their emissions through declining
emissions limits, or baselines, on a trajectory consistent with achieving Australia’s emission
reduction targets of 43% below 2005 levels by 2030 and net zero by 2050. Any new facilities
have their baselines determined using emissions intensity values set using international best
practice levels, recognising the opportunity to build world’s best practice emissions
performance into their design.
The Government’s Future Gas Strategy, echoed in the Net Zero Plan and Resources Sector
Plan, establishes that the role of gas will change as we move towards net zero in Australia by
2050. Australian gas will play an important role in an orderly global and domestic energy
transformation. Even in net zero scenarios, Australia and the world will need gas through to
2050 and beyond.
Our trading partners have indicated Australia will play a vital role in supplying the resources
needed to ensure their energy and economic security as they follow their own path to meet their
net zero commitments. The international demand for low emissions fuels and materials
underpins our Future Made in Australia ambitions. The Government is investing in the
establishment of new industries such as critical minerals processing and low carbon liquid fuels
to lower our emissions, attract investment and grow the economy.
Recommendation 8: State and federal governments must grant greater protections and
declare critical habitats for cetaceans, including Koontapool/Southern Right Whale.
The Government notes the recommendation.
This recommendation is not within the scope of the Bill.
The Southern Right Whale is listed as endangered and migratory under the Environment
Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999. In September 2023, the Government
publicly released updated Biologically Important Areas for the Southern Right Whale. This
includes areas used by the species that are important for migration and areas designated as
critical for reproduction. The National Recovery Plan for the Southern Right Whale was
finalised in 2024 in consultation with state agencies and species experts and includes current
scientific information on the species, habitat, identified threats and a range of conservation and
management actions required to mitigate potential impacts to the species.
Recommendation 9: Governments must ban seismic blasting and refuse any further fossil
fuel projects impacting Sea Country.
The Government does not support this recommendation.
On 9 December 2025, the Government published the revised Offshore Exploration Work Bid
Guidelines which included a requirement to apply a technology-neutral approach to seismic
acquisition and to minimise seismic surveying, where possible, to align with the Government’s
Future Gas Strategy.
5
The Government is committed to protecting our marine environment and enforcing safe and
environmentally responsible practices by the offshore resources sector. Offshore exploration
and development activities, including seismic surveying, are subject to a robust environmental
approvals regime, regulated by the National Offshore Petroleum Safety and Environmental
Management Authority (NOPSEMA). NOPSEMA employs qualified technical experts with
experience in offshore environmental impact and risk management, marine ecology and
environmental science.
Environment plans for proposed seismic surveys must demonstrate that any impacts and risks
associated with the activity have been evaluated, and that controls have been adopted to reduce
those impacts and risks to as low as reasonably practicable and to acceptable levels. If the
environmental impacts of a proposed seismic survey cannot be managed, NOPSEMA will not
allow the activity to proceed.
The Government’s Future Gas Strategy outlines a pathway for Australia to manage its evolving
role as a trusted gas producer and as a responsible climate actor which acts with transparency,
integrity, and from a strong evidence base. As the Future Gas Strategy states, gas will continue
to play an important role in Australia’s transition to net zero.
The Government recognises the importance of First Nations people’s connection to country and
is committed to improving social and economic outcomes through future energy projects.
Offshore titleholders must consult with relevant persons under the Offshore Petroleum and
Greenhouse Gas Storage (Environment) Regulations 2023, which may include Traditional
Owners and other cultural knowledge holders, during the preparation of an environment plan,
and demonstrate how impacts and risks will be mitigated.

Recommendation 11: Political donations from fossil fuel companies and other harmful
industries should be banned.
The Government notes this recommendation.
This recommendation is not within the scope of the Bill.
The Government has clear commitments to improve transparency and accountability across our
electoral system.
Following the 2022 election, the Government asked the Joint Standing Committee on Electoral
Matters (JSCEM) to inquire into and report on various matters, including political donations.
JSCEM did not recommend banning donations from specific donors. It did, however,
recommend the Government consider ways to provide greater transparency of political
donations.

In February 2025, the Government’s historic reforms to the electoral system to improve public
transparency and impose limits on political donations were passed by the Parliament. These
new laws will come into effect 1 January 2027.
The Government believes electoral reform should be undertaken in a consultative and
multipartisan manner and looks forward to continued engagement with colleagues across the
Parliament on electoral reforms.
