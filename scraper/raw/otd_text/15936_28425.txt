Overview
The Australian Government welcomes the report by the Senate Select Committee (the committee)
on the Tasmanian Freight Equalisation Scheme (TFES).
The Government recognises the importance of the efficient movement of goods and passengers
across Bass Strait to Tasmania’s broader economy and remains strongly committed to retaining the
TFES and the Bass Strait Passenger Vehicle Equalisation Scheme (BSPVES) to help offset shipping
costs faced by shippers and passengers across Bass Strait.
For many years successive Governments have provided significant support to Tasmania through the
TFES and the BSPVES. The schemes are designed to reduce the shipping disadvantage faced by
Tasmania and assist its producers to compete in other markets as well as alleviate the cost of sea
travel across Bass Strait. Collectively, the TFES and BSPVES have delivered more than $6 billion to
Tasmanian businesses since their introduction in 1976 and 1996 respectively.
For TFES shipments from 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2027, the rate of assistance has been increased by
25 per cent. This increase applies to all components of the scheme and delivers a real boost for
thousands of Tasmanian businesses. As the scheme nears the end of its fifth decade in operation,
the Government has also commissioned a comprehensive review of TFES to ensure it continues to
support Tasmanian businesses into the future.
As part of its inquiry into the TFES, the committee examined the merits and weaknesses of the
scheme and whether it is fit for purpose; if the scheme has kept up with increasing costs over the
past decade; shipping costs, competition and shipping industry competitive structures across Bass
Strait, including alternative freight options; eligibility criteria under the scheme; the operation and
administration of the scheme; and how the scheme impacts businesses on King Island and Flinders
Island. The committee has put forward five recommendations to improve the operation of TFES,
with additional comments made by the Chair, the Coalition Senators and the Australian Greens.
The Government’s response to the Senate Select Committee report on the Tasmanian Freight
Equalisation Scheme is set out in detail below.
The Government has made a commitment to work with the Tasmanian Government to boost
Tasmania’s competitiveness to provide more jobs and opportunities for Tasmanians.

Australian Government response
Recommendation 1:
4.17 The committee recommends that the Australian Government undertake a review of the
Tasmanian Freight Equalisation Scheme with consideration given to:
• the original policy intent of the Scheme and whether it remains fit for purpose;
• options to alleviate the freight cost disadvantage incurred by shippers of eligible non-bulk
goods between Tasmania, inclusive of the Bass Strait islands and the mainland; and
• the rates of assistance available and the class scale system.
The Government agrees with this recommendation.
The Government has commissioned a review of the TFES to examine options to improve, modernise
and enhance the Scheme, including the original policy intent of the scheme and whether it remains
fit-for-purpose.
The independent co-chairs for the review are Mr David Parmeter and Dr Jenny Gordon. Mr Parmeter
has a long history in the maritime industry, having previously served as President of one of the
world’s leading international shipping bodies – the Asian Shipowners Association – and as former
Chair of Maritime Industry Australia Limited. Dr Gordon has deep experience in economic and social
policy, previously serving as Chief Economist at Nous Group and the Department of Foreign Affairs
and Trade.
The review will consider the economic impact of the scheme, including the rates of assistance,
options for simplifying the scheme, and the competitiveness of sea and air freight trade on Bass
Strait and to the Bass Strait islands (incorporating Recommendation 5 within this broader review).
The terms of reference for the review are available at www.infrastructure.gov.au/infrastructure-
transport-vehicles/maritime/tasmanian-transport-schemes.
The review will also consider how the BSPVES is reducing the cost of moving passenger vehicles
across Bass Strait, and explore other issues raised in last year’s Senate Select Committee inquiry on
the TFES. A final review report and recommendations are due to the Government by mid 2026.
Since July 2025, Tasmanian businesses have been benefitting from a 25 per cent increase in TFES
assistance rates – putting more money in their back pockets at a time it’s needed most. The
estimated cost of this increased assistance is $93 million over two years.

Recommendation 2:
4.18 The committee recommends that, in the review of Government Business Enterprises currently
being conducted by the Tasmanian Government, the following factors be considered in
consultation with key port stakeholders:
• The primary role of TasPorts be; to provide the most efficient and cost effective facilitation of
trade; and
• The role of TasPorts as a shipping operator given the National Competition Policy
complications and additional costs generated.
The Government notes this recommendation.
TasPorts is a Tasmanian Government Business Enterprise and responsibility for this recommendation
resides with the Tasmanian Government.
Recommendation 3:
4.19 The committee recommends that Services Australia develop clear and succinct guidance
material and support services, including audiovisual user guides and instructions, and a
dedicated phone line, to support claimants to better understand their likely eligibility under
the Tasmanian Freight Equalisations Scheme to lodge accurate and timely claims.
The Government agrees in part with this recommendation.
The Government appreciates the feedback provided through the Senate Select Committee, and is
committed to the continuous improvement of customer experience.
A range of guidance material and support services to assist TFES claimants and staff is currently
available on the Services Australia Website at www.servicesaustralia.gov.au.
The Government will work to further refine website content, including the development of video
material where appropriate, to support claimants to better understand their eligibility, and lodge
accurate and timely claims.
A dedicated phone service for TFES is currently available to callers via the Services Australia National
Business Gateway line (131 158). Calls to this number are swiftly routed to the most appropriate
services officer skilled to manage their enquiry.

Recommendation 4:
4.20 The committee recommends that Services Australia develop an online claims calculator tool
with the functionality to estimate the approximate value of prospective claims to assist
prospective claimants to better understand their likely eligibility under the Tasmanian Freight
Equalisation Scheme.
The Government agrees in principle with this recommendation.
An online TFES calculator is readily accessible for approved users within the Centrelink Business
Online Services (CBOS) at www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/centrelink-business-online-services
authenticated environment. Functioning as a rate estimator, the online calculator accommodates
various claim types, including Domestic claims (pertaining to the TFES as well as King Island and the
Furneaux Group), Other Markets (for export claims), and Imported Goods (for imports).
The development of an online claims’ calculation tool accessible to all TFES claimants would be
contingent upon the outcomes of Recommendations 1 and 5 of this report.
Considering the age and lifecycle of the existing system, system modification should be considered
following a thorough review of TFES being undertaken, ensuring that any enhancements are
effectively aligned with the scheme’s future requirements.
Recommendation 5:
4.21 The committee recommends that the Australian Government commission the Australian
Competition and Consumer Commission to review the competitiveness of trade on Bass Strait
and Bass Strait islands considering both sea and air freight, including the rationale behind
pricing for the fresh produce sector.
The Government notes this recommendation.
The Government considers that it would be more efficient and effective for the competitiveness of
the Bass Strait trade to be considered as part of the broader review agreed to in recommendation 1
rather than undertaking two concurrent reviews.
Additional comments from the Chair, Coalition Senators, and the Australian
Greens
The Government notes the additional comments and recommendations provided by the Chair,
Coalition Senators, and the Australian Greens.
The Government considers that it would be more efficient and effective for these additional
comments and recommendations to be considered as part of the broader review agreed to in
recommendation 1.
