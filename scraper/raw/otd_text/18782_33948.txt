Introduction
The Australian Government welcomes the opportunity to respond to the House of
Representatives Standing Committee on Agriculture report Trading North: Inquiry into the
role of Australian agriculture in Southeast Asian Markets, tabled on 27 November 2024.
The Australian Government thanks all those who made submissions and appeared before
the inquiry.
The Australian Government recognises that strong and vibrant agricultural industries and
communities are integral to the economic, environmental, and social prosperity of Southeast
Asia. Rather than being in direct competition, Australia’s seasonality, terrain, and systems of
production complement those of Southeast Asian agriculture. Australia’s technical expertise
and agricultural proficiency is highly regarded by its regional neighbours, many of whom
readily see the potential for Australia’s quality produce and technical capability to be
transformative in uplifting their food security and improving livelihoods. Deepening
agricultural engagement in trade and investment, technology transfer, research for
development, exchange of best-practice approaches, and the sharing of leading-edge policy
solutions mutually benefits Australia and its Southeast Asian partners.
Stronger agricultural partnerships between Australia and Southeast Asian countries foster
more profitable, resilient, and sustainable agricultural industries and rural communities both
at home and abroad, while underwriting Australia’s relationship capital and strategic
engagement in the region.
The Australian Government’s response below and addresses the recommendations
contained in the report. Any funding for the programs and initiatives referenced in this
response is a matter for further Government consideration in future Budget process.
Response
Recommendation 1
The Committee recommends that the Australian Government continue to support the
implementation of the Moore report recommendations.
Supported
Invested: Australia’s Southeast Asia Economic Strategy to 2040 (Invested) highlights the
opportunities and benefits from enhanced agriculture and food trade and investment in
Southeast Asia. Complementarity in natural resource endowment, production synergies and
geographic proximity are powerful tailwinds for further deepening commercial engagement
for shared economic advantage. The Government is taking action to advance Invested,
including through new and expanded initiatives to implement its recommendations.
Implementing Invested: Australia's Southeast Asia Economic Strategy to 2040 provides an
update of progress since the launch of Invested on 11 October 2023.

Recommendation 2
The Committee recommends that the Australian Government provide its agencies with the
resources required to:
• Maintain Australia’s agricultural trade with Southeast Asia.
• Revise trade agreements as required.
• Address non-tariff barriers to trade.
• Harmonise regulations and standards to streamline trade, including supporting the
traceability of Australian agricultural products.
Supported
The Australian Government is committed to harnessing the full potential of its trading
relationship with Southeast Asia, its second largest agricultural trading bloc and a region for
which food security is a priority. In 2024-25 around 19% of Australia's agricultural exports,
worth $14.8 billion, went to Southeast Asia with exports up 40% since 2020-21.
DFAT has initiated a comprehensive review of Australia's free trade agreements (FTAs) in
Southeast Asia to assess market access, address emerging issues, and support trade
diversification. This work is complemented by the $1.75 million Southeast Asia Free Trade
Agreements (FTAs) Modernisation Grant Program for Australian industry to identify trade
and investment priorities and harness future opportunities in the region.
Non-tariff barriers (NTBs) represent significant impediments to Australian exports across the
region. DFAT leads a whole-of-government NTB Strategy aimed at eliminating or reducing
NTBs impacting Australian exports. DFAT works closely with DAFF and other agencies to
address NTBs impacting agricultural exports through bilateral, regional and multilateral
mechanisms including representations by overseas posts and raising trade concerns in the
WTO Sanitary and Phytosanitary (SPS) and Technical Barriers to Trade (TBT) committees.
The Government is also collaborating with Southeast Asian countries on trade rules and
standards harmonisation under the ASEAN-Australia-New Zealand Free Trade Area, the
Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership, Asia-Pacific Economic Co-operation and
through Government-funded initiatives between Australian and Southeast Asian standards
bodies.
The $3.9 million National Agricultural Traceability Grant Program (June 2024 to September
2026) ‘Building trust in Australian agricultural traceability and credentials in Southeast Asia’
was an investment in 11 projects to foster collaborative agricultural traceability projects
within the Southeast Asian region to both strengthen and promote trust in Australia’s
commercial agricultural traceability.

Recommendation 3
The Committee recommends that the Australian Government support the trade in organic
agriculture in Southeast Asia by:
• Improving accessibility and detail of information provided on the MICOR and
Austrade websites, with a specific focus on organic operators.
• Providing a dedicated point of contact officer for Asian markets, with a background
in the organic industry and a thorough understanding of the requirements for
organic export.
• Ongoing budgetary funding to improve resources available for the organic industry.
• Providing organic-specific grant programs tailored to support organic operators
seeking entry into the Southeast Asian market.
• Reviewing the domestic regulatory framework to ensure its alignment with current
international standards and potential rival jurisdictions, including mechanisms
within the framework to define and regulate organic products within Australia’s
domestic market.
Noted
The Australian Government recognises the potential for growing Australian exports of
organic products to Southeast Asia as consumer incomes rise. DAFF is continually updating
its MICOR database to improve trade information and reflect new market access
opportunities, including for organic products, and its technical and policy teams work with its
overseas Agriculture Counsellor network to identify and unlock new trading opportunities for
Australian exporters including organics exporters.
The Australian Government has provided funding to support organic agricultural exporters
grow their businesses through the Australia-Vietnam Enhanced Economic Engagement
Grant and Export Market Development Grants (2021). Australian Organic Ltd has also been
selected to participate in Austrade’s Trade Diversification Network as part of the Australian
Government’s $50 million Accessing New Markets Initiative (2025 two-year surge program).
The Australian Government monitors developments in international organic standards to
assess their alignment with Australia’s regulatory framework for organics, and has
responded to recent inquiries on domestic settings including the inquiry into the National
Organic Standard Bill 2024.
DAFF has five dedicated Agriculture Counsellors located in key Southeast Asian markets
supported by DAFF technical and policy staff onshore that provide advice to Australian
exporters on market access requirements and negotiate favourable trading conditions with
overseas governments. This is supported by Austrade’s Go Global Toolkit which offers
online export information and advice to help Australian businesses expand overseas and
capitalise on Australia’s free trade agreements.

Recommendation 4
The Committee recommends that the Australian Government make a long-term
commitment to the Southeast Asia Business Exchange program to strengthen
engagement and collaboration with Australia’s partners in Southeast Asia, with a particular
emphasis on agriculture.
Supported
The Australian Government has committed funding of $19.2 million from 2023-24 (and $5.3
million per year ongoing) to establish the Southeast Asia Business Exchange (SEABX).
SEABX conducted 17 business missions across different Southeast Asian markets and
sectors as of 31 June 2026, including two missions to Indonesia/Brunei and
Vietnam/Cambodia focused on the agrifood sector.
Recommendation 5
The Committee recommends that the Australian Government invest in the development of
Southeast Asian literacy within the business community and the education system to
strengthen the relationship between Australia and its partners in Southeast Asia.
Supported
Strengthening Southeast Asia literacy within the Australian business community and
education system is an Australian Government priority, consistent with Recommendation 1
of Invested - ‘developing a whole-of-nation plan to strengthen Southeast Asia literacy in
Australian business, government, the education and training system, and the community’.
Since the October 2022-23 Budget, the Australian Government has committed more than
$90 million to build Southeast Asian literacy within the Australian business community and
the education system, this includes:
• $33.1 million over five years from 2023-24 (and $8.2 million per year ongoing) to
establish an ASEAN-Australia Centre in Canberra to deepen engagement with
Southeast Asia
• $19.2 million over four years from 2023-24 (and $5.3 million per year ongoing) to
establish the Southeast Asia Business Exchange, supporting Australian businesses
to enter and expand in Southeast Asian markets
• $14.9 million over four years from 2023-24 to extend the National Centre for Asia
Capability (Asialink Business), supporting Australian businesses to deepen their
engagement in the region
• $12.9 million over three years from 2024-25 for new scholarships and fellowships
under the Aus4ASEAN program
• $6.0 million over four years from 2023-24 to establish a pilot program of placements
and internships for young professionals in Australian and Southeast Asian
companies
• $4.0 million over four years from 2022-23 to establish a pilot in-country language
skills program in Vietnam

Recommendation 6
The Committee recommends that the Australian Government provide more funding to
maintain and enhance the Agriculture Counsellor network in Southeast Asia; and provide
additional resources for the technical staff required to undertake technical negotiation and
trade cooperation activities.
Noted
The Australian Government recognises that the Agriculture Counsellor network provides
invaluable support for Australia’s agricultural industries, helping overcome market entry
barriers and enabling more Australian products to be successfully exported to Southeast
Asia. The work of Agriculture Counsellors is guided by, and dependent upon, policy and
technical staff in Australia who provide strategic direction and technical expertise to ensure
Australian producers meet importing country requirements.
DAFF’s Agriculture Counsellor network plays an essential role in supporting the work of
DAFF’s technical market access negotiators by offering market and tactical insights and
establishing invaluable connections with key decision-makers in foreign governments. The
network supports the navigation of local regulations, assists DAFF technical officers in
resolving detained shipments at overseas ports, pinpoints market opportunities, and bolsters
bilateral and multilateral agricultural relationships.
The 2026-27 Budget provided support for DAFF’s agricultural trade and export functions
including its agriculture counsellor network.
• $45.1 million over four years from 2026–27 (and $11.4 million per year ongoing) to
continue Australia’s international engagement in agricultural forums and trade
standard setting functions.
• $23.8 million over four years from 2026–27 (and $6.1 million per year ongoing) to
continue to support access to critical global agricultural markets.
• $8.2 million in 2026–27 to maintain export regulatory services, with revised cost
recovery arrangements for these services deferred to 1 July 2027 in recognition of
the disruptions being experienced by farmers and producers due to the conflict in the
Middle East.
• $2.97 million in 2024-2025 to 2027-2028 financial years from the Department of
Foreign Affairs and Trade - Southeast Asia and Australia Government-to-
Government Partnerships Program to collaborate with Partner Country Agencies to
improve sustainable agriculture and food security practices, to strengthen biosecurity
cooperation with Indonesia by supporting sustainable biosecurity practices and
capacity-building activities and identify opportunities to deliver upon strategic
priorities with other Partner Countries, in Southeast Asia.

Recommendation 8
The Committee recommends that the Australian Government continue to fund and
resource the work of the Cairns Group and the Cairns Group of Farm Leaders, and the
Special Representative for Australian Agriculture.
Noted
The Australian Government is strongly committed to advancing Australia’s national
agricultural trade interests through active engagement in international systems that help
Australian farmers to access export markets, enhance Australia’s leadership on agricultural
trade, build alliances with like-minded countries and industry; and address non-tariff matters
in multilateral forums.
Australia is permanent Chair of the Cairns Group, a coalition of 20 agricultural fair-trading
countries, which together account for almost 30 per cent of the world’s agricultural exports.
Its membership is comprised of both developed and developing members, including five
Southeast Asian nations – Indonesia, Philippines, Thailand, Malaysia, and Vietnam.
Thailand became the Cairns Group inaugural rotating Vice Chair in 2024. Through the WTO,
the Cairns Group works to liberalise agricultural trade, discipline export subsidies and
eliminate trade-distorting domestic support.
With food security clearly in focus, the Cairns Group’s advocacy underpins environmentally
sustainable agri-food systems, improved rural livelihoods, and economic development. In the
lead-up to and during and after the 14th Ministerial Conference in Cameroon, and against a
backdrop of increasing protectionism by some WTO members, Australia as Cairns Group
Chair is committed to leading resistance to the erosion of existing WTO agricultural
disciplines and driving trade-positive reform. The Australian Government will continue to
encourage the Cairns Group’s Southeast Asian members to serve as an influential voice for
the region, recognising that more open and less distorted world markets for agriculture will
foster greater regional and global prosperity and security.

Recommendation 9
The Committee recommends that the Australian Government increase the resources
available for capability building programs for the food and agriculture sector within the
Southeast Asian region, and that additional funding to relevant agencies be provided on a
long-term basis.
Noted
Australia is recognised for its world-class expertise in food safety, plant and animal sciences,
biosecurity, and sustainability. Southeast Asian partners value this expertise which is directly
relevant to modernising their agricultural sectors. Southeast Asian markets each differ in
their structure, level of complexity, and commercial potential for Australian agrifood
businesses that warrants a nuanced approach.
The Southeast Asia and Australia Government to Government Partnerships program
(SEAG2G), announced in the 2023-24 Budget, is a 5-year, $50 million initiative helping
Australia and Southeast Asia deepen institutional cooperation. Sustainable agriculture and
food security is one of its four pillars of SEAG2G, building joint policy and technical capability
and advancing the objectives of Invested.
The Australian Government also funds other Official Development Assistance programs in
Southeast Asia to foster regional agriculture prosperity and food security, including through
the Australian Centre for International Agricultural Research. In 2024, DAFF initiated a three-
year project on ‘Evidence-based Sustainable Agriculture Research and Engagement’,
involving research collaborations and outreach with ASEAN stakeholders to build and
deepen trusted relationships over time.
Recommendation 10
The Committee recommends that the Australian Government increase the resources
available for regional collaboration on biosecurity with Southeast Asia.
Noted
The Australian Government continues to examine opportunities to bolster regional
collaboration on biosecurity within current budget settings.
The Government recognises that building partnerships beyond Australia’s borders
contributes to a stronger Australian biosecurity system by reducing biosecurity risks at
source. Building the biosecurity capacity of countries in Southeast Asia supports their
economic resilience and creates a frontline of biosecurity defence for Australia. Biosecurity
collaboration continues to underpin regional disease prevention, emergency response
planning, and the implementation of disease control programs, including through the
provision of vaccines, improved information systems, and enhanced laboratory capacity.
Australia’s ongoing investment in biosecurity capacity building across Southeast Asia
fortifies bilateral relationships and builds strategic trust with counterpart agencies. This
regularly provides a platform for progressing trade and market access outcomes with
overseas competent authorities given the overlap of technical experts involved in biosecurity
protection and technical negotiations on trade in plant and animal products.

Recommendation 11
The Committee recommends that the Australian Government provide additional funding to
the Australian Centre for International Agricultural Research for its work in Southeast Asia.
Noted
ACIAR has built agricultural research-for-development partnerships in Southeast Asia since
1982 to address challenges in food production systems and natural resource management.
It has extensive networks within partner countries’ research ecosystems, and its research
has had a significant impact on evidence-based policy and capacity development.
An assessment of 40 years of investment in partner countries yielded a benefit cost ratio of
43:1, with several Southeast Asian countries generating the highest net benefits from
investments including new germplasm for grain and tree crops, grain storage, plantation
timber, and pest and disease management in horticultural crops.
Enhancing the productive and technical capacity of our regional neighbours strengthens
Australia’s international reputation, and collaborative research also benefits Australia’s
innovation system and agriculture and natural resource industries. As Southeast Asia’s
research systems mature, there is an ongoing need to support food systems research and
build capability across issues, including animal and plant biosecurity, agriculture
sustainability, climate change mitigation and adaptation, innovation in agriculture, and value-
chain equity and efficiency.

Recommendation 13
The Committee recommends that the Australian Government investigate opportunities for
employment, knowledge, and skills transfer, using an extension of the PALM scheme into
the Southeast Asian region.
Not supported.
The PALM scheme is highly valued by the Pacific and Timor-Leste and is strategically
important to Australia’s engagement with the region, as well as for our economy.
The Pacific Australia Labour Mobility (PALM) scheme allows eligible Australian employers to
hire workers from participating countries to fill specified roles when there are not enough
local workers available. Eligible businesses can recruit workers in unskilled, low-skilled, and
semi-skilled positions in a range of sectors including agriculture and select agriculture
related food product manufacturing sectors.

communities back home.
