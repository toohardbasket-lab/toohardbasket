    Introduction   

    The Australian Government welcomes the opportunity to respond to the Senate Legal and Constitutional Affairs Legislation Committee’s report,        Administrative Review Tribunal (Miscellaneous Measures) Bill 2024     (Committee report) tabled on 31 October 2024.   

    The Administrative Review Tribunal (Miscellaneous Measures) Bill 2024 (    Bill    ) form    ed         the final legislative element of the reform tha    t abolished the Administrative Appeals Tribunal (AAT) and established the Administrative Review Tribunal (Tribunal), a new federal administrative review body that is user-focused, efficient, accessible, independent and fair. The Tribunal commenced operation on 14 October 2024    .   

    The Bill ma    de     further amendments to 52 Commonwealth Acts (including the        Administrative Review Tribunal Act               2024     (ART Act)    ), to update references to the AAT in legislation that passed the Parliament since the introduction of the     Administrative Review Tribunal Bill 2024    ,     and     to     make technical amendments to support the efficient conduct of Tribunal review and ensure the legislation operates as intended.     The Bill     passed the Parliament on 12         February         2025    , and the         Administrative Review Tribunal (Miscellaneous Measures) Act 2025         (Act)     received Royal Assent on 20 February 2025    .   

    The Government’s response to the Senate Committee report is set out below. The response addresses the recommendation    s     in the report, and in     the     Australian Greens’ dissenting report. The Government also acknowledges the additional comments made by Senator Scarr included in the Committee report.

   Dissenting report by Australian Greens   



          Recommendation 1                        

         The government should amend the bill to introduce a full fee waiver for financially vulnerable individuals, including those in immigration detention and victims of domestic, family, or sexual violence. There should also be consideration of extending payment periods for those unable to pay within the standard time frames.       

    

     The Government        does not agree            with this recommendation.   

    

     T    he     reform to Australia’s system of federal merits     review, and the subsequent commencement of the Tribunal         on 14     October 2024    ,         include    d     a range of amendments which         seek         to     improve the     Tribunal’s accessibility, including     requiring the Tribunal to conduct its proceedings in a way that is accessible for the parties, taking into account their needs (s    ee section     51     of the     ART Act). T    he President     also     has the power     under the ART Act     to     make practice directions in relation to     the     accessibility     and responsiveness of the Tribunal     to the diverse needs of parties to proceedings     (see paragraph 36(1)(k))    .   

    

     The migration system is a specialised and distinct part of government decision-making. The reform significantly harmonise    d     the powers and procedures that are available to the Tribunal when conducting reviews of reviewable migration and protection decisions.     The         Migration        Act         1958     (Migration     Act    )    ,     and the        Migration Regulations 1994     (Migration     R    egulations)     as amended during the     broader     reforms     to Australia’s system of federal merits review    ,     retains the     previous     fees fo    r     AAT     review     of     migration and protection visa     decisions     –     with         one exception    . As part of the reform’s objective to improve accessibility, the Migration Regulations were amended     to lower the threshold for reduction of the fee     for     review from ‘severe financial hardship’ to requiring only ‘financial hardship’    .   

    

     C    ertain cohorts     are     exempt from     the requirement to     pay an application fee, or     are     eligible to pay a reduced fee     in concessional circumstances    .     This may include victims of domestic, family, or sexual violence.     Under     the Migration     R    egulations, an applicant can seek a fee concession of 50% in circumstances of ‘financial hardship’    .         Further,         applicants for review of a decision not to grant a protection visa do not have to pay any fees to make a valid application, and a claim for review of a protection visa cannot be denied on the basis that the fee ha    s     not been paid.   



          Recommendation 2   

                         

           1.8 The government should amend the bill to ensure the required documentation and information are reasonable, clear and do not impose excessive burdens on applicants, especially those in detention who often have difficulties accessing documents.      

      

       The Government            notes     this     recommendation    .   

         

       G    overnment     amendments to the Bill     removed     item 115–119, which related     to     the requirements for an application for review by the Tribunal of a reviewable migration or protection decision.   

      

       As outlined in the Explanatory Memorand    a to the Bill as introduced and the Government amendments    ,     these items     were intended to clarify     the operation of the     existing     law regarding     what constitutes a ‘properly made’ application to the Tribunal for the review of migration or protection decisions    .         In this way, they were intended to provide greater certainty to the Tribunal and applicants by clarifying the minimum requirements that would enliven the Tribunal’s jurisdiction    .         They were not intended to amend     or alter the intent of the existing law.   

      

       Noting concerns raised during Parliament’s consideration of the Bill, t    he Government     removed     these amendments         to     ensure there was     additional time to consider     existing settings within    ,     and     the approach to amending     relevant     requirements under    ,     t    he Migration Act.   

         


         Recommendation 3   
                     

         1.10 Harmonise application timeframes for both detained and non-detained applicants, removing the seven-day restriction for those in detention or alternatively, provide additional time for applicants facing barriers to access legal services or translation.       

  The Government               notes         this recommendation.   

  Government amendments made to the         Bill     in the Senate extend the timeframe in which a person in immigration detention must apply to the Tribunal for a review from 7 days to 14         days after the person is notified of the decision. This ensures that applicants in immigration detention would not     have     a shorter timeframe     to apply     than they     were         afforded     before the commencement of the amendments to the Migration Act contained in the        Administrative Review Tribunal (Consequential and Transitional Provisions No. 1) Act 2024    .   

  The         reform standardised     the majority of     timeframes for     decisions under the         M    igration Act     to be consistent with Tribunal     timeframes for making an application for review    . There are     two exceptions     to this    , one of which is when a person is in immigration detention.   

  The Government’s position remains that     it is not feasible to remove this exception and     it would not be appropriate to     harmonis    e     application timeframes for both detained and non-detained applicant    s    . T    he shorter timeframe     is required     to     minimise     the amount of time a person spends in detention     and to maintain the overall integrity of Australia’s     m    igration system    .         T    he only action required from an applicant     withi    n this timeframe is to make a valid application    .   

   It is the Government’s policy to reduce the amount of time people spend in detention. I    t would not be appropriate to extend certain application timeframes as this     c    ould lead to people spending more time in immigration detention.   



         Recommendation 4   

                         

           1.12 Grant the Administrative Review Tribunal discretion to accept applications with minor or procedural or factual errors or omissions if it serves justice, ensuring vulnerable applicants are not disadvantaged by technicalities or minor omissions. This is clearly in the overall interest of the efficient and fair administration of justice.      

    

     The Government        does not agree            with     this     recommendation.   

    

     If an     application is not     made         in accordance with current legislative requirements – that is,     ‘    properly made    ’ –     the Tribunal will not have jurisdiction to review the decision.     As noted in the Government’s response to recommendation 2 of the dissenting report,     above,     the Government introduced amendments to the Bill     to remove items 115–119         relating to the requirements for a ‘properly made’ application to the Tribunal.     The    se         items were no    t     intended     to     change         existing     requirements     for making     a ‘properly made’ application         to the Tribunal,     and the consequences of not making a ‘properly made’ application    .   

    

     The Government remains of the view that     it     is important that the    re is     clarity and certainty as to when a    n a    pplication     has     been validly made         to give certainty to     applicants and the Tribunal    ,     and     to     ensure the integrity of the migration system.         The     migration     system requires certainty about a person’s visa status at all times, to ensure there is clarity around a person’s review rights and their status as a lawful or unlawful non     citizen    .   

    

        Recommendation 5   
                    
     1.14 Align procedural standards for migration and protection reviews with those used in other Administrative Review Tribunal areas, including the flexibility to extend lodgement timeframes for applicants facing extenuating circumstances.      


     The Government        does not agree         with this recommendation.   

    

     Consistent with the     Governmen    t’s response to recommendation 3    ,     above,         the migration system is a specialised and distinct part of government decision-making.         While the reform has taken significant steps to harmonise Tribunal procedures across its jurisdiction, some provisions retain and enhance special features that are fundamental to the operation of Australia’s migration system. This recognises the distinct nature of these applications and their volume and complexity.   

    

     As     noted in     the     Attorney-General’s D    epartment    ’s response to this issue when raised during the Committee’s inquiry into the Administrative Review Tribunal Bill 2023 [Provisions] and related bills –in Question 10 of document 20 in the list of answers to questions on notice (available on the         inquiry website     )     –         applicants are not able to seek extensions of time to make an application because the migration system requires certainty about a person’s visa status at all times. Without a valid visa, a person is an unlawful non-citizen, must be detained in immigration detention and is expected to depart from Australia.   

    

      If the Tribunal were able to grant an extension of time for making an application for review of these decisions, it becomes more difficult to determine the end of the application period. This would be incompatible with how the migration system works, and particularly with decisions about a person’s status as a lawful or unlawful non-citizen, and removal of unlawful non     citizens from the country     .
