Overview
The Universities Accord (Student Support and Other Measures) Bill 2024 (the Universities Accord
Bill) and the Higher Education Support Amendment (Response to the Australian Universities Accord
Interim Report) Bill 2023 respond to recommendations from the Australian Universities Accord (the
Accord) and the Australian Universities Accord Interim Report.
The Education and Other Legislation Amendment (Abolishing Indexation and Raising the Minimum
Repayment Income for Education and Training Loans) Bill 2022 was introduced by the Australian
Greens Party.
Preparation of Government response
In line with the Department of Prime Minister and Cabinet’s (PM&C) tabling guidelines, the
Australian Government is not required to respond to recommendations to pass, not pass, or amend
a Bill, as this is a matter for the Senate.
Education and Employment Legislation Committee report into the Universities Accord (Student
Support and Other Measures) Bill 2024
The Government supported the recommendation of the Senate Education and Employment
Committee to pass the Universities Accord Bill.
Additional comments were made by Greens Senators which included three recommendations.
Education and Employment Legislation Committee report into the Higher Education Support
Amendment (Response to the Australian Universities Accord Interim Report) Bill 2023
The Government supported the recommendation of the Senate Education and Employment
Committee to pass the Accord Interim Report Bill.
A dissenting report made by Coalition Senators provided five recommendations.
Additional comments were made by Greens Senators which included 11 recommendations
numbered 2 – 12.
Education and Employment Legislation Committee report into the Education and Other Legislation
Amendment (Abolishing Indexation and Raising the Minimum Repayment Income for Education and
Training Loans) Bill 2022
The Government supported the recommendation of the Senate Education and Employment
Committee not to pass the Indexation Bill.
Additional comments were made by Coalition Senators which included one recommendation.
A dissenting report made by Greens Senators included five recommendations.

Australian Government response
The Accord was the biggest and broadest review of the higher education sector in 15 years. It sets
out a blueprint for higher education reform for the next decade and beyond.
High and growing student debt was recognised by the Accord as a financial burden on individuals
that is exacerbating current cost of living pressures. The Accord found that negative perceptions of
growing student debts may discourage future students from participating in higher education.
To address these concerns, the Albanese Labor Government has delivered a 20 per cent cut to
student debts incurred on or before 1 June 2025, benefiting more than 3 million Australians. It
particularly helps younger Australians, with around 70 per cent of people repaying a student loan
being 35 or younger. The reduction is part of broader changes to make the student loan repayment
system fairer and delivers significant and immediate cost of living relief to Australians with student
debt.
The Accord also recommended reducing the financial burden of repayment of education related
debts on low-income earners, and limiting disincentives to work additional hours by moving to a
system of HELP repayment based on marginal rates. To address this, the Government has introduced
a fairer repayment system for HELP debts and other student loans, by increasing the minimum
repayment threshold from $54,435 in 2024-25 to $67,000 in 2025-26 (which will continue to
increase each year with the growth in wages).
The Government has introduced a marginal repayment system where compulsory student loan
repayments are calculated only on income above the new $67,000 threshold rather than having it
based on a percentage of their repayment income.
Helping more Australians into a home is one of the Government’s highest priorities. People with a
student loan should be treated fairly when they want to buy a house. The Government asked
regulators and banks to review guidance on student loans to make it easier for them to responsibly
take out a mortgage and buy a home. The Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) has made
targeted changes to how banks treat student loan repayments when assessing home loan applications.
APRA has issued revised reporting standards and a prudential practice guide to make it fairer and
easier for those with a student loan to get into their own home.
This builds on the significant reforms the Government has already implemented. In 2024, the
Government wiped $3 billion of student debts and fixed the system so that the indexation on
student debts can never increase faster than wages. The Government has also established new
Commonwealth Prac Payments to support about 73,000 eligible teaching, nursing, midwifery and
social work students while they are completing their compulsory practical training at university or
through VET. The Government has also expanded Fee Free Uni Ready courses to help more students
from disadvantaged backgrounds to get a chance at going to university.
Through the Fee-Free TAFE Skills Agreement, the Government, in partnership with state and
territory governments, is delivering a combined investment of over $1.5 billion for 500,000 Free
TAFE and VET places across Australia from 2023 to 2026.
The Government has also made Free TAFE a permanent feature of the national VET system, investing
over $1.6 billion to 2034–35 to support at least 100,000 places per year from 2027, underpinned by
the Free TAFE Act 2025.
The Government has removed the previous Coalition government’s punitive ’50 per cent Pass Rule’,
which did not include requirements for higher education providers to improve the quality of
education or offer other supports to assist students to successfully complete their studies. This
requirement disproportionately impacted students from low socio-economic backgrounds, First
Nations students and other students from under-represented or educationally disadvantaged
cohorts.
3
The Government replaced this unfair Rule with a requirement for higher education providers to have
a Support for Students Policy. This strengthened accountability and reporting requirements for
higher education providers to ensure students are properly supported to study and to pass. It also
ensured higher education providers that fail to meet the new requirement would be subject to
compliance action, including possible financial penalties.
The Government has passed legislation that guaranteed all First Nations students (excluding
medicine), regardless of location, a Commonwealth Supported Place when accepted into their
chosen course of study. In 2024, First Nations commencements increased by 7 per cent and total
First Nations enrolments increased by more than 5 per cent. In 2026, the Government extended this
to every First Nations student who meets the requirements to study medicine. Growing the First
Nations medical workforce is an important step in improving healthcare outcomes for First Nations
communities.
The Government has moved to provide greater support and flexibility for higher degree by students
through the Higher Education Support (Commonwealth Scholarships) Guidelines 2025 (CSGs) which
commenced on 3 December 2025. The CSGs include the requirement for higher education providers
(HEPs) to provide an increased minimum paid parental leave entitlement for Research Training
Program (RTP) stipend scholarship recipients who are the primary carer of a child.
In 2025, Commonwealth and State and Territory Education Ministers established an Expert Council
on University Governance to develop new Principles and Recommendations to strengthen university
governance. Education Ministers have considered the Expert Council’s Principles and
Recommendations to inform actions the Government is taking to improve university governance.
This includes embedding Principles in regulations and requiring universities to report against them
on an ‘if not, why not?’ basis, alongside other steps to improve accountability, transparency,
engagement and representation on university governing bodies.
The Australian Government has established the independent National Student Ombudsman (NSO) to
investigate and resolve student complaints about universities. The NSO commenced operations on
1 February 2025 and is providing guidance to universities to improve their complaints-handling
practices. The NSO has strong investigative powers and can make recommendations to a provider
about the actions that should be taken to resolve a complaint. The NSO has the power to monitor a
provider’s implementation of its recommendations, as well as share information with relevant
regulators to provide the basis for potential compliance action where necessary.
If a provider does not take appropriate action in response to NSO recommendations, the NSO is able
to provide the Minister for Education with a copy of an investigation report for tabling in Parliament.
The NSO reports annually on complaint volumes, complaints outcomes and compliance with
recommendations.
Additionally, the Australian Government has passed legislation to establish the National Higher
Education Code to Prevent and Respond to Gender-based Violence (National Code). This landmark
reform sets 7 clear, legally enforceable standards that higher education providers must meet to
effectively prevent and respond to gender-based violence, including in student accommodation. The
Australian Government has established the Higher Education Gender-based Violence Regulator to
hold higher education providers accountable for compliance with the National Code. The National
Code commenced on 1 January 2026.
