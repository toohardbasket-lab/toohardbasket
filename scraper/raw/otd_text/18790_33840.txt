Overview
On 25 August 2025, the Senate referred the quality and safety of Australia’s early childhood
education and care system matter to the Senate Education and Employment References Committee,
for inquiry and report by 10 March 2026 (extended to 31 March 2026).
The Senate Education and Employment References Committee reported on the quality and safety of
Australia’s early childhood education and care (ECEC) system on 31 March 2026.
The report makes 23 recommendations.
Labor Senators submitted a dissenting report with one recommendation.
The Australian Greens Senators submitted a dissenting report with 13 recommendations.
Australian Government Response
National Child Safety
In August 2025, Education Ministers across the country agreed on historic national actions to
strengthen safety and quality in early childhood education and care. Backed by around half a billion
dollars from governments, these are the biggest safety reform to the sector since the national early
learning system was introduced 15 years ago.
Every child deserves access to quality early learning – and these reforms are about making sure early
learning is safe, high quality, and delivered in services families can trust.
A key part of this national action is the investment of up to $45 million to establish the National
Early Childhood Worker Register (National Worker Register). The foundational stage of the National
Worker Register rolled out nationally on 27 February 2026 and requires approved providers to
record details of their workforce including identifying details, Working with Children Check (WWCC)
and relevant qualifications and training. As of 1 October 2026, around 98% of services have
uploaded worker details to the National Worker Register.
Enhancements to the National Worker Register will be rolled out progressively to enable it to reach
its full potential in supporting child safety and enabling earlier identification of risks. These
enhancements could include verification of worker identity and other credentials, and enhanced
worker screening functionality to enable worker information to be shared with WWCC (and
equivalent agencies) and prospective early education and care employers.
The foundation course of the National Child Safety Training was launched on 27 February 2026 and
as of 1 October 2026 has been completed by 408,433 individuals. This training focused on building
an understanding of child safety as well as identifying child abuse and neglect and promoting
appropriate action and referral. It must be completed by any person who works or volunteers in an
ECEC service regulated under the National Quality Framework, including CEOs and board members
who make decisions relating to a service. Around 97 per cent of individuals surveyed after
completing the training have said they would recommend it.
The next stage of training became available from 31 July 2026 and must be completed by all persons
with management or control of the service, nominated supervisors, persons in day-to-day charge,
and all staff who work directly with children. This training builds on the foundation training to equip
workers with the skills they need to confidently and consistently implement child safe practices.
2
Both national training courses were developed by the Australian Centre for Child Protection (ACCP),
in partnership with the Queensland Government, on behalf of all Australian states and territories
and the Australian Government.
The Australian Government is also providing $21 million for wage-subsidy grants that will support
completion of the training. The subsidy helps ECEC providers pay staff while they undertake training.
Small and medium-sized providers will be prioritised in allocating the funding.
Alternatively, providers can close after 5pm for up to 5 hours annually and claim the Child Care
Subsidy while their staff undertake the mandatory safety training.
A National Assessment of CCTV has also been undertaken in more than 300 ECEC services (the
Assessment). The Assessment has examined how CCTV may support the detection, prevention and
response to harm, as well as regulatory oversight and public confidence.
The Assessment has been informed by experts including the Australian Federal Police (AFP), the
ACCP, the National Office for Child Safety, the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, as
well as other leading child protection, privacy, and security experts, alongside specialist advisors in
surveillance, privacy law and child safety. Findings will be presented to Education Ministers in
November 2026 and will inform future policy on the safe, ethical and effective use of CCTV in ECEC
settings.
Education Ministers have agreed and implemented reforms to the Education and Care Services
National Law and Regulations (National Law) – the national system of early childhood education
regulation – to strengthen safety and quality in ECEC. The reforms include:
• banning use of personal mobile phones and other electronic devices in services;
• improving transparency for families by enhancing the information available about compliance
and enforcement actions on Starting Blocks;
• tripling penalties for non-compliance with National Law;
• expanding the powers of regulatory authorities to respond to misconduct;
• strengthening Working with Children Check requirements; and
• making the safety, rights and best interests of children the paramount consideration under the
National Law.
Education Ministers tasked the Australian Children’s Education and Care Quality Authority (ACECQA)
with undertaking a rapid assessment of child supervision practices in ECEC. All recommendations
from ACECQA’s Rapid Assessment of Child Safe Practices were accepted by Education Ministers.
Reforms to address the recommendations will be part of the next tranche of reforms to the National
Law.
The Australian Government has invested around $22 million for the Joint Compliance and
Monitoring Program (JCMP) with the States and Territories. The JCMP enables joint regulatory
action, including data and intelligence sharing, joint investigations and monitoring, identification of
emerging sector risks and trends, as well as enhanced deterrence and enforcement through co-
ordinated action across jurisdictions.
The National Quality Framework (NQF) Snapshot for Quarter 2 of 2026 shows that state and territory
regulators conducted more inspections than ever before – 6,493 service visits, up 5.6 per cent on the
previous quarter and 8.7 per cent on the prior year. State and territory regulators also took 1540
3
compliance actions, an increase of around 110 per cent compared with the same time last year, and
up 65.1 per cent on the previous quarter.
Strengthening Regulation of Early Education
In 2025, the Australian Government passed new laws to hold providers to account – changing Family
Assistance Law to give the Commonwealth the power to suspend or cancel Child Care Subsidy
funding where a service is not meeting quality, safety or compliance requirements.
These laws give the Secretary of the Department of Education the power to take into account a
provider’s quality, safety and compliance history when considering whether a provider should be
approved to administer the Child Care Subsidy (CCS), whether they should continue to be approved,
or if they should be approved to operate a new service. As of 1 October 2026, 127 services have
been put on notice; 59 of these services are now Meeting Quality Standards while 10 are no longer
approved (including where a provider has surrendered their licence). The powers have been used to
cancel the CCS-approval of two providers.
The legislation also gave the Department the power to undertake unannounced service visits to
ensure the CCS is being administered correctly and to support early identification of safety and
quality issues to the relevant state or territory regulator. The Australian Government has invested
$73 million to support implementation of this new power. As of 1 October 2026, more than 1,500
unannounced service visits had been undertaken across all states and territories (since November
2025).
Working with Children Checks
In November 2025, all Attorneys General endorsed the Standing Council of Attorneys General (SCAG)
Agreement to Deliver National Working with Children Check Reform (WWCC SCAG Agreement).
The WWCC SCAG Agreement outlines six Priority Actions that set out the agreed pathway and
timeframes to strengthen and improve consistency for a national approach to WWCCs, based on
mutual recognition:
1. implementing mutual recognition of negative notices
2. meaningful consistency in automatic exclusion criteria
3. meaningful consistency in risk assessment frameworks
4. clear information sharing pathways
5. an operational National Continuous Checking Capability (NCCC)
6. mutual recognition of WWCCs.
Significant progress has been made across Priority Actions under the WWCC SCAG Agreement, with
jurisdictions continuing to work collaboratively to strengthen and improve national consistency in
WWCC arrangements. On 31 July 2026, all jurisdictions agreed the mid-2026 deliverables set out in
the WWCC SCAG Agreement, establishing meaningfully consistent baselines of disqualifying
offences, risk assessment processes and the types of information to be shared with state and
territory WWCC screening units. By early 2027, jurisdictions are to amend legislation and policy as
required to reflect the agreed approach, with mutual recognition of WWCCs to be achieved by mid-
2028.
Early Education and Care Commission
In May 2026, the Australian Government announced it will work with states and territories to
consider the development of an Early Education and Care Commission (Commission).
4
In July 2026, Education Ministers agreed to explore the development of a potential Commission to
strengthen safety and quality in the ECEC system, and that if established, it should involve reform of
the existing Australian Children’s Education and Care Quality Authority (ACECQA).
A Working Group co-chaired by the Australian and South Australian Governments has been
established to provide advice to Ministers. Education Ministers have also agreed to establish a time-
limited expert panel to support targeted consultation on a potential Commission and will consider
further advice in November 2026.
Worker Retention Payment
On 17 June 2026, the Australian Government announced a further investment of $3.6 billion over
the next 2 years to continue funding a wage increase for the ECEC workforce. This investment is tied
to a commitment from services receiving the payment to limit fee increases, and a requirement that
they meet Quality Area 2 (Children’s Health and Safety) of the National Quality Standard from July
2027.
Workforce retention and stability is critical to quality and safety in ECEC, enabling children and
educators to develop consistent and stable relationships. Since the Worker Retention Payment was
announced in 2024:
• there are around 20,000 more ECEC workers, about an 8 per cent increase
• job vacancies have decreased by around 30 per cent
• the percentage of services operating with a staffing waiver has fallen from 8.9 per cent to
4.3 per cent
• fees at centres receiving the payment grew at around half the rate of centres that are not
signed up.

Recommendation 1
The committee recommends that the Government prioritise the closer integration of law
enforcement into current child safety reforms, to allow a more joined-up approach to child safety.
Specifically, the committee recommends the government consider:
a. as a priority—closer integration of the WWCC register, the National Educator Register and
police/regulator intelligence;
b. reforms to allow the use of facial recognition tools for the purpose of rapid victim
identification by police in cases of child sexual assault, subject to appropriate legislative
guardrails and oversight mechanisms commensurate with the sensitivity of the technology
and the privacy interests engaged, and noting its status as an emerging technology;
c. establishing appropriate mechanisms, whether operational, structural or otherwise, to allow
regular two-way feedback between police and regulators in relation to sub-threshold
matters; and
d. establishing clear guidance on the preservation of CCTV footage and other records for
evidentiary purposes in cases involving alleged child sexual abuse, including appropriate
retention periods, access protocols, and safeguards to ensure that any such requirements do
not result in the surveillance of children beyond what is necessary for child protection
purposes.
The Australian Government partially supports this recommendation, and notes that work is already
underway in collaboration with states and territories in areas where responsibility is shared.
The Australian Government’s response, as outlined above, highlights action through the WWCC
SCAG Agreement to progress closer integration of WWCC systems with police and regulatory
intelligence, as well as other sources of jurisdictional and Commonwealth information.1
The Australian Government’s response also details the work underway through the Education
Ministers Meeting to enhance the National Early Childhood Worker Register and to explore the use
of the CCTV in ECEC settings through a National Assessment. Findings from the Assessment will be
presented to Education Ministers in November 2026 and will inform future policy on the safe, ethical
and effective use of CCTV in ECEC settings.

Recommendation 5
The committee recommends that the Australian Government amend the Fair Work Act 2009 to
ensure that employment settings appropriately reflect the safeguarding requirements of child-
7
facing workplaces, including early childhood education and care, and protect employers who, in
good faith and on reasonable grounds, take adverse employment action (including suspension,
role reassignment, or termination) due to concerns about a worker’s potential risk to children.
The Australian Government notes this recommendation.
The Fair Work Act 2009 already allows employers to take disciplinary action against workers who
pose safety risks to children. In early childhood education and care settings, approved providers
have legislative obligations under the Education and Care Services National Law to protect children
from harm and ensure children are adequately supervised. Providers may suspend staff or take
other reasonable action to manage child safety risks while allegations are being investigated.
The adverse action provisions of the Fair Work Act 2009 do not prevent employers from taking
reasonable action to address genuine child safety concerns, including where action is necessary to
protect children or manage risks while allegations are investigated. Such disciplinary action will not
fall within the meaning of adverse action unless it is taken for a prohibited reason, such as the
employee has exercised a workplace right or because of an employee’s protected attribute, such as
race. Employers must, however, act fairly and provide the reasons for their action.
Recommendation 6
The committee recommends that the Australian Government review how national child safety
training is delivered and assessed in early childhood education and care sector to ensure it drives
genuine engagement with safeguarding.
This should include strengthening assessment requirements so that participants demonstrate a
clear understanding of the material, and examining whether current settings adequately require
engagement and robust evaluation, with ongoing monitoring incorporated into regular audit
processes. The review should ensure the training functions as a real safeguard that builds
capability and awareness, rather than a procedural exercise.
The Australian Government supports in principle this recommendation, and notes collaboration
between states and territories to roll out the National Child Safety Training. The recommendation
relates to matters where the Australian Government shares responsibility with states and territories
or where states and territories have sole responsibility.
As the National Child Safety Training is still being rolled out, a review of the training would be
premature at this time. However, further to the response above, the Australian Government notes
that:
• the training was developed in consultation with the sector and experts to ensure that it
supports all people involved in ECEC to meet their legal child safety obligations, strengthen
safeguarding practice, and reduce harm to children;
• the training requires the learner to undertake an assessment for each topic in the course in
order to progress;
• the training imposes progression controls, randomised question banks and mandatory
knowledge checks to encourage engagement and ensure learners demonstrate a clear
understanding of the material;
• National Law requires approved providers to ensure all staff undertake the training and state
regulators are auditing compliance;
8
• the ACCP collects feedback on user experience and engages in ongoing improvement and
may use this feedback to review and update the materials where required; and
• the training was first updated on 10 June 2026 to implement changes from feedback
received.
Recommendation 7
The committee recommends that the Australian Children’s Education and Care Quality Authority,
in consultation with relevant peak bodies, develop and implement guidelines for anaphylaxis
prevention and management for children with allergies; and guidelines for the appropriate safety
and care of children with type 1 diabetes.
The Australian Government notes this recommendation. The recommendation relates to matters
where the Australian Government shares responsibility with states and territories or matters where
states and territories have sole responsibility.
The Education and Care Services National Regulations require services to have policies and
procedures regarding medical conditions in children, including anaphylaxis. Approved providers
must ensure that staff and volunteers understand both the medical conditions policy, and each
child’s medical requirements.
ACECQA provides support in implementing the National Allergy Council’s Best Practice Guidelines for
Anaphylaxis Prevention and Management in Schools and Best Practice Guidelines for Anaphylaxis
Prevention and Management in Children's Education and Care. The guidelines outline the key
principles and recommendations for reducing the risk of anaphylaxis in ECEC settings, including and
outside school hours care.
The Australian National Diabetes Strategy 2021–2030 (the Strategy) sets out a national approach to
the prevention, early detection, and management of diabetes in Australia. Within this broader
framework, the Strategy recognises the particular needs of children with type 1 diabetes,
emphasising the importance of ongoing, well-coordinated care that is appropriate to their stage of
development.

Recommendation 9

Recommendation 12

The Australian Government supports in principle this recommendation.

The committee recommends that the Australian Government, as a matter of urgency:
• Publish grant guidelines and confirm the application opening date for the professional
development subsidy ahead of the commencement of mandatory national child safety
training obligations for new staff on 14 August 2026;
• Provide clear public guidance on the total funding available under the subsidy, the allocation
methodology, and the treatment of training costs incurred by providers prior to the opening

• Ensure that the subsidy administration process does not disadvantage providers (particularly

Recommendation 12
That the Australian Government address inclusion in early childhood education and care by:
• Immediately increasing funding to the Inclusion Support Program to meet immediate unmet
need and while the Service Delivery Price project reaches its conclusion, including increasing
the additional educator hourly rate to meet award levels. The subsidy cap should also cover a
child’s full attendance of hours at a service;
• Over the short term create an Early Childhood and Education Fund with both a disability
specific stream and mainstream inclusion support stream;
• Over the medium term develop and implement a needs-based funding model to support the
inclusion of children with additional needs and developmental delay; and
• Collate data on how many children with disability are denied enrolment in ECEC services or
supported through the Inclusion Support Program.
The Australian Government notes this recommendation.
The Australian Government acknowledges the importance of inclusion of all children in early
education and care. The 2026-27 Budget provides a further $54.8 million in supplementary funding
