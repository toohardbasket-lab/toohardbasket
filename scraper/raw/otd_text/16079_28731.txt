    Introduction   

    The Australian Government welcomes the opportunity to respond to the Senate Legal and Constitutional Affairs Legislation Committee’s report,        Administrative Review Tribunal Bill 2023 [Provisions] and related bills     (Committee report) tabled on 13 May 2024.   

    The package of Bills has now passed the Parliament. The        Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024     (ART Act) received Royal Assent on 3 June 2024. The        Administrative Review Tribunal (Consequential and Transitional Provisions No. 1) Act 2024     (Consequential and Transitional Act 1) and the        Administrative Review Tribunal (Consequential and Transitional Provisions No. 2) Act 2024     (Consequential and Transitional Act 2) received Royal Assent on 31 May 2024.     These Acts abolished the Administrative Appeals Tribunal and established a new federal merits review body – the Administrative Review Tribunal (Tribunal). The Tribunal commenced operation on 14 October 2024.    

    T    hese Acts represent the most significant reform to Australia’s system of administrative review in decades    , giving     effect to the recommendations from the Senate Legal and Constitutional Affairs Reference Committee’s 2022 inquiry into the performance and integrity of Australia’s administrative review system.    

    The Acts establish a strong, user-focused administrative review body that will improve the quality and transparency of decision     making across government. To this end, the ART Act also gives effect to a number of recommendations of the Royal Commission into the Robodebt Scheme (Robodebt Royal Commission), including – critically – the re     establishment of the Administrative Review Council.   

  

      The Government’s response to the Senate Committee report is set out below. The response addresses the recommendations in the report, and in Senator Scarr’s dissenting report. The Government acknowledges the additional comments made by the Australian Greens included in the Committee report.    


     Recommendations    


          Recommendation 1                 
  The committee recommends that the Senate pass the bills.      

     
  The Government        agrees to         this recommendation.    

  The Bills passed the Senate on 16         May         2024. The ART Act received Royal Assent on 3 June 2024. Consequential and Transitional Act 1 and Consequential and Transitional Act 2 received Royal Assent on 31 May 2024.     The ART Act, and most provisions within the Consequential and Transitional Acts    ,     commenced on 14 October 2024.   



    Recommendation 2                 

         The committee recommends that, on the establishment of the Administrative Review Council, the Australian Government should refer to the Council the amendments to the         Migration Act 1958        in the Administrative Review Tribunal (Consequential and Transitional Provisions No. 1) Bill 2024 and the matters raised in evidence to this committee regarding the operation of the Administrative Review Tribunal in relation to migration and asylum matters.      



    Recommendation 3                 
  The committee recommends that, following the establishment of the Administrative Review Council, the Australian Government should also consider referring to the Council the operation of the social security-related provisions as set out in the Administrative Review Tribunal Bill 2024 and the Administrative Review Tribunal (Consequential and Transitional Provisions No. 1) Bill 2024, having regard to the findings of the Robodebt Royal Commission.      

     
     The Government         agrees        to the Committee’s recommendation       s 2 and 3       .        

  The re-established Administrative Review Council will consider and report on the operation of the administrative law system. The Council’s functions include:   


             monitoring the integrity of the Commonwealth administrative review system   


             inquiring into and reporting on systemic challenges in administrative law, and   


             supporting education and training for Commonwealth officials in relation to administrative decision making and the administrative law system.    

      
  The Council may on its own initiative or at the request of the Minister inquire into, prepare a report on, and make recommendations to the Minister on matters relating to any of the Council’s functions.     The Council is an independent body and has discretion as to whether to inquire into a matter.   


         The former Attorney        General, the Hon Mark Dreyfus KC MP, wrote to the Chair of the Council        on 4 March 2025       , drawing the Committee’s report to the Council’s attention.        Following that correspondence, the Council        has decided to        conduct inquiries into:   


          the operation of the social security provisions with the ART        A       ct and     Consequential and Transitional Act 1, due for report by 30 June 2026, and   


        the     operation of the migration-related provisions within the     ART Act and     Consequential and Transitional Act     1, due for report     in December 2026.   

   

     


    Dissenting report by Senator Paul Scarr   



    Recommendation 1                 
  The case for abolition of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal and its replacement with the Administrative Review Tribunal has not been made out. Reforms to the merits review process should be made through amendments to existing legislation rather than through abolition of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal    .      


              The Government         does not agree               with this recommendation       .   


          


              The        legislation to repeal the         Administrative         Appeals         Tribunal Act 1975        and establish the new Tribunal has now been enacted.        The Tribunal commenced operation on 14 October 2024.   



    Recommendation 2                 
  As is the case in various state-based merits review tribunals, the ART bills should provide discretion for the Administrative Review Tribunal to award costs to the applicant in exceptional circumstances.      



              The Government         does not agree               with this recommendation.   


          


              T       he issue of awarding costs was considered as part of consultation and policy development that informed the final form of the legislation. The Government ultimately determined that there are more effective tools to address concerns around Government party behaviour in merits review proceedings that do not raise the practical challenges and logistical complexities               that would accompany the Tribunal having discretion to award costs.   


          


              The Tribunal is by default a no-cost jurisdiction, meaning each party bears their own legal costs. This        was the        default position in the AAT        and        is a continuation of the status-quo for merits review. The reform has maintained existing arrangements for costs across the Tribunal’s jurisdiction.   


          


              In certain types of Tribunal decisions, the Tribunal can order that a party is liable to pay another party’s costs. These circumstances are specifically allowed in the relevant legislation       , and apply in the types of decisions to which costs orders are suited.               F       or example, subsection               67(8) of the         Safety, Rehabilitation and Compensation Act                 1988        empower       s        the        Tribunal        t       o make an order that the costs of the proceedings incurred by the claimant, or part of those costs, be paid by the responsible authority.   


          


              Examples of a generalised power to award costs to the applicant in exceptional circumstances in other Tribunals        a       re        rare       . Where State and Territory tribunals have powers to award costs in exceptional circumstances, they can do so against any party. In the context of the reform and other changes to address agency engagement with Tribunal reviews, the        G       overnment considers it preferable to maintain existing arrangements. However, this could be an area for future consideration by the Administrative Review Council.   


          


     Recommendation 3                 
  Consideration be given to removing     ‘    without prejudice    ’     privilege from clause 30 because the obligation to produce documents subject to such privilege may act as a deterrent to the resolution of matters through without prejudice negotiations.      


                The Government         does not agree               with this recommendation.   


           


               The dissenting report includes extracts of the        Attorney        General’s Department’s (d       epartment       )        response to this issue when raised in written questions on notice following the department’s appearance at a public hearing of the Committee on 26 April 2024. The position reflected in these extracts remains the Government’s position in relation to the inclusion of ‘without prejudice’ privilege in section 30        of the ART Act       , t       hat is,        that        section 30:   


               does not prevent or disincentivise, parties from negotiating settlements after an application has been made to the Tribunal   


               only applies to the initial production of documents to the Tribunal       , and    


               does not apply to documents obtained by the decision-maker after the review has commenced       .   


           


               T       he Government notes that s       ection               30        of the ART Act        replicates        the effect of        subsection               37(3) of the AAT Act. Abrogating these privileges ensures that the Tribunal has access to the same materials as the decision-maker when making the reviewable decision. This is a key component of merits review, allowing the Tribunal to ‘stand in the shoes’ of the decision-maker.   


           


               Throughout the reform process, the department worked in close consultation with the AAT, among other stakeholders, to ensure any concerns over existing settings could be addressed through the course of the reform. The inclusion of ‘without prejudice’ privilege in section 30 of the ART Act was not raised as a potential deterrent or disincentive for parties to negotiate a settlement. This is largely due to the nature and practical sequencing of the administrative decision        making process, whereby parties would not typically engage in discussions that would be considered ‘negotiations’ until after the primary decision has been made.   



    Recommendation 4                 
  Clause 66(3) be amended to provide for procedural fairness, including a right for a representative to be provided with adverse information and to respond prior to the Administrative Review Tribunal making an order to remove them.      


              The Government                 does not agree               with this recommendation       .   


          


              However, the Government        notes the power of the President to make practice directions which may include guidance for members in relation to the removal of a person’s representative.   


          


              Consistent with the department’s response to this issue – provided for in Question 4 of document 20 in the list of answers to questions on notice (available on the          inquiry website        )        – the Government notes that        the ART        Act        requires that all parties and their representatives use their best endeavours to assist the Tribunal to make the correct or preferable decision, and to achieve the objective in        section        9 of the ART        Act       .   


          


                The AAT        had        an implied ability to remove representatives where they        were               not assisting the Tribunal, and        did so previously       . The ART        Act        makes this power explicit and ensures that it can only be used where the Tribunal considers that the representative:   


             has a conflict of interest in representing the person   


             is not acting in the best interests of the person   


             presents a safety risk to any person   


             presents an unacceptable privacy risk to any person, or   


             is otherwise impeding the Tribunal.   


          
  The Tribunal is required to observe procedural fairness when exercising powers to remove a person’s representative (which could include providing the representative with adverse information and an opportunity     to     respond).     Because the Tribunal is required to act with procedural fairness in all its decisions, the ART Act does not explicitly import procedural fairness requirements into each individual provision. However, m    ore specific guidance for Tribunal members on     how procedural fairness should be afforded to representatives in the exercise of discretions under section 66     could be provided for in the Tribunal’s Practice Directions (made under     section     36).   

  The note to     section     66     also     clarifies that if the Tribunal orders the removal of a person’s representative, that person may choose another representative. Depending on the circumstances of the removal, the person might also elect to have the removed representative as a support person, rather than a representative to proceedings.   



    Recommendation 5                 
  That clause 71(2)(d)(ii) should be redrafted to specify the type of harm to a person that is relevant to the Administrative Review Tribunal    ’    s consideration of whether to hold a hearing in private, or for the non-publication or non-disclosure of certain information.      


              The Government         does not agree                with this recommendation.   


          
  The settings provided for in the     ART     Act     respond to feedback received     during     consultation that the prospect of having one’s personal information made public could be a deterrent, for some individuals, to seeking review of an administrative decision.   

  When making confidentiality or non-disclosure orders, the Tribunal must balance the principles of open justice and procedural fairness with the circumstances of each case     and any harm that is likely to occur to a person if an order is not made    . Depending on the individual’s circumstances, harm could take many forms and it is ultimately for the Tribunal to exercise their discretion to address the unique circumstances of each individual.   


          
  The     Government’s position remains that     Parliament cannot envisage or predict all the types of harm that may result to a person in unforeseeable circumstances.     As with the Government’s response to recommendation 4 of the dissenting report, above, m    ore specific guidance for Tribunal members on this matter could be provided for in the Tribunal’s Practice Directions (made under section         36).   



     Recommendation 6                 
  Part 6 of the ART Bill and related provisions should be referred by the Government to the INSLM for review, to ensure that an appropriate balance is struck between national security, fairness and transparency objectives.      

  The Government         notes         this recommendation.   

  The Government notes that t    he Independent National Security Legislation Monitor (INSLM) may review the operation, effectiveness and implications of Australia    ’    s national security and counter‑terrorism     legislation and related     laws.   

  The Government acknowledge    s     the comments made by some stakeholders during the Parliamentary scrutiny process, including by the Law Council, that Part 6 of the ART Bill should be referred to the INSLM.     T    hese reforms demonstrate the Government    ’s     commit    ment     to ensuring our foundational institutions, and the legislative frameworks they operate within, deliver fairness and justice for all users.   

  The     Government     does not consider a referral to the INSLM is necessary, noting the INSLM can conduct a review of its own initiative. The         Government reiterates that the     approach provided for in Part 6 of the ART     Act     strikes the appropriate balance between providing applicants with procedural fairness and promoting public safety and national security by ensuring adequate protections are in place for the handling and review of sensitive information.   



    Recommendation 7                 
  As a matter of good public policy, any compensation for loss of office should be calculated in accordance with the determination of the independent Remuneration Tribunal. That principle should apply with respect to all offices, including any compensation for loss of office which is required to be paid under the ART Bills.      


              The Government                 does not agree               with this recommendation.   


          
  Transitional arrangements in Consequential and Transitional     Act     1 provide that the Remuneration Tribunal (Compensation for Loss of Office for Holders of Certain Public Offices) Determination 2018 does not apply in relation to the person’s appointment as a member of the AAT     (    item 32, Schedule 16, Consequential and Transitional     Act         1    )    .   

  Instead, full-time AAT members who     were         not appointed to the Tribunal     were to         be paid an amount equivalent to their salary for 4 months, or, if there     was         less than 4 months remaining on their term of appointment, for their remaining term. Depending on a member’s level, the compensation amount for any one individual     was     between $78,973 and $176,877.   

  As the     former     Attorney     General    ,        the Hon Mark Dreyfus KC MP,     noted in the Second Reading Speech     when introducing the Administrative Review Tribunal (    Consequential and Transitional     Provisions No. 1)     Bill     2023    , these arrangements ensure that compensation payments are fair and reasonable but not excessive    , and – importantly – in line with community expectati    ons.
