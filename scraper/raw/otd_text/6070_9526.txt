Australian Government response to the
Senate Legal and Constitutional Affairs Committee report:
Migration Amendment (Prohibiting Items in Immigration Detention
Facilities) Bill 2020
APRIL 2024
Recommendations
Committee Recommendations
Recommendation 1:
2.74 The committee recommends that the government make administrative
arrangements that ensure detainees have access to communication facilities that will
reasonably meet their needs, and enable timely, and where appropriate, private contact
with friends, family, and legal services.
Response:
The Government notes this recommendation. However, given the passage of time since the
report was tabled, a substantive Government response is no longer appropriate.
Recommendation 2:

Labor Party Senators’ Dissenting Report
The ALP dissenting report recommended that the Bill not be passed unless the below
recommendations were agreed to.
Recommendation 1:
Labor Senators recommend that the Government either withdraw the bill, or
significantly amend the Bill to address these concerns and ensure that the bill is focused
on the specific risks and does not impose broad sweeping measures that punish
detainees who themselves may be at risk from other, high risk detainees.
Response:
The Government notes this recommendation. However, given the passage of time since the
report was tabled, a substantive Government response is no longer appropriate.
Recommendation 2:
Labor Party Senators recommend that the bill be amended to define 'prohibited thing'
more clearly and that it be defined in legislation to enable appropriate parliamentary
oversight.
Response:
The Government notes this recommendation. However, given the passage of time since the
report was tabled, a substantive Government response is no longer appropriate.
Recommendation 3:
Labor Party Senators recommend that the Department of Home Affairs’ instructions to
their staff and Serco staff on how to implement the minister’s written directions
concerning ‘prohibited things’ be contained in disallowable instruments.
Response:
The Government notes this recommendation. However, given the passage of time since the
report was tabled, a substantive Government response is no longer appropriate.
Recommendation 4:
Labor Party Senators recommend that the bill be amended to ensure that detainees are
not prevented from possessing or using electronic devices such as mobile phones unless
there is evidence that removal is necessary and proportionate.
Response:
The Government notes this recommendation. However, given the passage of time since the
report was tabled, a substantive Government response is no longer appropriate.
Recommendation 5:
Labor Senators recommend that, if the Government revises the legislation to be more
targeted, it also implements measures which provide extra protections for staff working
in immigration facilities, especially those health and medical professionals in
immigration facilities.
Response:
The Government notes this recommendation. However, given the passage of time since the
report was tabled, a substantive Government response is no longer appropriate.
Recommendation 6:
Labor Party Senators recommend that all parties to migration detention matters agree
to use the same published data to avoid confusion in debate and in policy development.
Response:
The Government notes this recommendation. However, given the passage of time since the
report was tabled, a substantive Government response is no longer appropriate.
