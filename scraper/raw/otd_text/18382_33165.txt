Australian Government response to the Joint Committee on Corporations and Financial Services Report
Introduction
The Government thanks the Parliamentary Joint Committee on Corporations and Financial Services
for its work on the prevalence and impact of, and potential responses to, financial abuse in Australia.
The Government recognises financial abuse as a complex and insidious form of violence that may be
perpetrated within the context of family and domestic violence or elder abuse, as well as within
other relationships, such as carer relationships.
The Government sincerely thanks the individuals and organisations who made submissions and
provided evidence over the Committee’s public hearings, in particular, those victim-survivors who
shared their personal experiences with the Committee.
The Committee’s report outlines the growing evidence that perpetrators of family and domestic
violence are exploiting government and financial systems to control, threaten or harass a current or
former partner, often as part of a pattern of coercive control and financial abuse.
Around 16 per cent of women and 7.8 per cent of men in Australia have experienced economic abuse
from a current or previous partner.1 This can cause economic hardship, costly and lengthy legal
proceedings, and emotional and administrative burdens for victim-survivors, making it difficult to
rebuild their lives after leaving an abusive relationship.
Ending financial and economic abuse in all its forms requires sustained and coordinated effort at all
levels of the community and government to prevent it from happening, hold perpetrators to account
and support those who have experienced or are experiencing violence.
The Government’s actions to help end financial abuse
Working for Women: a national strategy for gender equality (2024)
Working for Women: a national strategy for gender equality (Working for Women) is the
Government’s 10 year strategy for advancing gender equality in Australia. Its five priority areas
include ending violence against women, aligned with the National Plan to End Violence Against
Women and Children 2022-32, and advancing women’s economic equality and security.
Working for Women acknowledges the connections between safety and economic security and the
need to work across all priority areas to advance gender equality. Working for Women includes
addressing financial abuse, including in government systems, among its future actions, and its
reporting framework tracks experience of economic abuse. Working for Women is the framework for
whole of government policy and structural reform to advance gender equality.

2 | Introduction
National Plan to End Violence against Women and Children 2022–2032
The National Plan to End Violence against Women and Children 2022–2032 (National Plan) outlines
the Government’s vision to end gender-based violence in one generation, and provides the national
policy framework for ending gender-based violence in Australia, guiding the work of Australian, state
and territory governments, family safety experts and front-line services. The National Plan identifies
economic abuse including financial abuse as a common form of family and domestic violence and a
key area of focus for addressing gender-based violence in Australia.
Audit of Australian Government Systems
The Government is working to embed safety and stamp out opportunities for systems abuse,
including financial abuse, within government systems. Following the National Cabinet meeting on
6 September 2024, the Government commenced an audit of key Australian government systems to
identify areas where they are being weaponised by perpetrators of family and domestic violence, in
order to stamp out opportunities for systems abuse.
Work is underway across government to address the findings from the audit so far and
to identify interventions and system improvements that can protect and support victim-survivors of
gender-based violence. As an immediate response to the audit, the Government has committed to
practical reforms to embed safety in Commonwealth systems and close loopholes exploited by
perpetrators of financial abuse. These include:
• Preventing perpetrators from using tax and corporate systems to create debts as a form of
coercive control and making perpetrators accountable for these debts if they do.
• Looking at how to stop perpetrators of domestic and family violence from receiving their
victim’s superannuation after death.
• Looking at making perpetrators liable for social security debts incurred by a victim-survivor
due to coercion or financial abuse.
Further details of these reforms are referred to in greater detail throughout the body of this
Government Response.
The audit is an ongoing mechanism to examine existing government systems and identify how they
can be strengthened to improve safety for victim-survivors and prevent further abuse. The
Government is also working to apply the principles of Safety by Design into systems reform and
design to contribute to preventing systems and financial abuse.
Implementing recommendations of reviews in relation to financial abuse
Financial abuse, particularly in government systems, has been the subject of further investigations
since the Committee handed down its report, including by the Commonwealth Ombudsman’s report
Weaponising Child Support: when the system fails families (2025) and the Tax Ombudsman’s report
Identification and management of financial abuse within the tax system (2025).
The Government is implementing the recommendations of these reports to improve the
management of financial abuse in the child support and taxation systems.

Response to the recommendations
Recommendation 1
That the Australian Government establish a mechanism for co-design with victim-survivors of financial
abuse (including through representative groups) in relation to the implementation of legislative,
regulatory and sector-driven reforms aimed at mitigating the prevalence and impact of financial abuse,
including the recommendations of this report.
Australian Government Response
The Government supports this recommendation. The Government has a range of stakeholder
engagement mechanisms which emphasise the value of lived experience in the development of
policy relating to financial abuse and other forms of family and domestic violence, as well as a broad
range of other stakeholders. These include the Financial Elder Abuse Project; the Lived Experience
Advisory Council; and the Systems Abuse Priority Reforms Stakeholder Engagement Forum. The
Government will continue to engage with these groups, and to centre the experience of victim-
survivors of family and domestic violence in the development of financial abuse related policy.
Recommendation 2
That the Australian Government amend the National Consumer Credit Protection Act 2009 and ASIC’s
Regulatory Guide 209 to specifically require:
- that the lender must take reasonable steps to be satisfied that a borrower and any guarantor is not
experiencing financial abuse; and
- that the lender must take reasonable steps to verify the lending requirements and objectives of each
borrower and any guarantor.
Recommendation 3
That the Australian Government establish a review of Responsible Lending Obligations and the National
Credit Code, informed by the lived experience of victim-survivors, to consider options for:
- protecting victim-survivors of family and domestic violence to obtain credit; and
- the inclusion of specific conditions to support victim-survivors of family and domestic violence to stay
in their own homes.

Recommendation 4
That the Australian Government amend the National Credit Code to require financial institutions to
inform all borrowers of changes to joint credit contracts in circumstances of reasonably suspected
family and domestic violence, including financial abuse.
Australian Government Response
The Government supports recommendations 2,3 and 4 in principle.
The Government recognises the important role that consumer credit laws, lending practices and
financial institutions play in identifying, preventing and responding to financial abuse. The evidence
before the Committee highlighted the ways in which credit and lending arrangements can be
misused by perpetrators to facilitate coercive control, create financial liabilities, or further
disadvantage victim-survivors of family and domestic violence.
The Government agrees that lending frameworks should appropriately support victim-survivors and
reduce opportunities for financial abuse, and notes that any changes to responsible lending
obligations, consumer credit requirements or notification arrangements must be carefully designed
to ensure they are effective in practice, proportionate, and do not create unintended consequences,
including opportunities for perpetrators to further exploit or manipulate financial systems.
The Government will continue to consider options to address the intent of these policy proposals
through collaborative work with industry, consumer advocates, victim-survivor representatives and
relevant regulators, including the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC). This work
will be informed by ongoing consultation and consideration of lived experience to ensure any
measures strengthen protections for victim-survivors, support access to appropriate credit, and
cannot be readily manipulated or weaponised by perpetrators of family and domestic violence.
As part of this work, the Government will consider opportunities to strengthen responses to financial
abuse within existing consumer credit and regulatory frameworks. The Government will also consider
how reforms in this area align with broader efforts to address systems abuse and embed safety
across financial and government systems.

Recommendation 5
That the Australian Government consider how to best allow financial institutions to document and/or
flag actual or suspected financial abuse against their customers when detected or reasonably suspected,
including without explicit consent from customers. This may include amendments to the Privacy Act
1988.
Australian Government Response
The Government supports this recommendation.
The Government acknowledges the intent of this recommendation and recognises the importance of
enabling financial institutions to identify and respond appropriately to actual or suspected financial
abuse. The ability to safely record, flag and share relevant information can assist financial institutions
to better support victim-survivors and reduce opportunities for ongoing abuse.
The Government is considering options to address this issue as part of a second tranche of reforms to
the Privacy Act 1988. On 31 August 2026 the Government released draft legislation and an
accompanying consultation paper on privacy reforms. These consultations respond to the
committee’s recommendation by proposing amendments to the Privacy Act's existing "permitted
general situation" for serious misconduct. The reforms would broaden the exception from
"misconduct of a serious nature" to "wrongdoing of a serious nature", recognising that it may apply
to forms of financial abuse, including where a person is exploiting a customer's vulnerability or
misusing an enduring power of attorney.
The proposal would also provide greater clarity that financial institutions can collect, use and disclose
personal information where they reasonably suspect serious wrongdoing and reasonably believe the
handling is necessary to take appropriate action, helping to facilitate earlier detection and response
to suspected financial abuse.
In considering these proposals, the Government will continue to engage with relevant stakeholders
to ensure any reforms appropriately balance privacy protections with the need to support and
protect people experiencing financial abuse. The Government will also consider how any proposed
measures can be designed to minimise unintended consequences and ensure that information-
sharing or flagging mechanisms cannot be manipulated or weaponised by perpetrators to cause
further harm to victim-survivors.

Recommendation 6
That the Australian Government amend the Credit Reporting Code to specify that financial abuse is
considered ‘circumstances beyond the individual’s control’; and that the Australian Retail Credit
Association (ARCA) develop a best-practice financial abuse guideline for credit reporting bodies and
credit providers.

The Government supports this recommendation in principle.
The Government recognises the importance of ensuring that victim-survivors of financial abuse are
not unfairly disadvantaged within the credit reporting system as a result of conduct perpetrated
against them.
The Credit Reporting Code was amended in 2024 to clarify that domestic abuse, including financial

The Government will continue to monitor their implementation and effectiveness and will consider
whether further changes are required as part of its consideration of the Review of Australia's Credit
Reporting Framework. This will include consideration of opportunities to strengthen protections for
victim-survivors and promote consistent industry responses to financial abuse across the credit
reporting system.

The Government supports this recommendation in principle.
The Government recognises that family violence, elder abuse and homelessness can pose significant
risks to an individual's safety and wellbeing, and that appropriate information-sharing settings can
play an important role in supporting early intervention, risk management and access to services.

Recommendation 9
Recognising the legitimate choice of Australians to have self-managed superannuation funds, the
committee recommends that the Australian Government undertake a review of the intersection
between financial abuse and the superannuation system, particularly in relation to self-managed
superannuation funds; and ensure that the review is informed by the lived experience of victim-
survivors.
Australian Government Response
The Government supports this recommendation in principle.
The Government recognises the importance of understanding how financial abuse can intersect with
the superannuation system, including within self-managed superannuation funds (SMSFs). Evidence
provided to the Committee highlighted concerns that superannuation arrangements may, in some
circumstances, be exploited by perpetrators of financial abuse as a means of exerting control,
restricting access to assets, or undermining a victim-survivor's long-term financial security.
The Government agrees that the experiences of victim-survivors should inform consideration of
measures to strengthen protections within the superannuation system. The Government will
continue to consider opportunities to improve safeguards against financial abuse, informed by
engagement with regulators, industry, victim-survivors and relevant stakeholder groups.
As part of broader reforms to improve consumer safety in the superannuation system, the
Government announced a range of reforms impacting SMSFs on 19 August 2026. These measures
include providing the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) with additional powers to prevent or delay
superannuation rollovers into SMSFs where there are indicators of financial abuse, scams or other
misconduct, and to engage directly with trustees and members where concerns arise. These powers
are intended to support early intervention and reduce the risk of irreversible financial harm.
In addition, the Government is introducing mandatory education requirements for SMSF trustees.
These requirements will help ensure trustees better understand their obligations and responsibilities,
and provide an additional safeguard against misconduct, poor decision-making and the exploitation
of vulnerable individuals.
The Government considers that these reforms represent important steps towards addressing the
issues identified by the Committee. The Government will continue to monitor emerging risks and
consider whether further action is required to strengthen protections against financial abuse within
the superannuation system, including in relation to SMSFs.

appropriately support victim-survivors and their families and align with broader efforts to prevent
and respond to financial abuse.
The Government has consulted on reforms to prevent perpetrators of family and domestic violence

perpetrator from benefiting from the superannuation death benefits of their victim in specified

In developing these reforms, the Government has extended the policy to include defined benefit
schemes, recognising the importance of establishing protections across different parts of the
superannuation system. The Government will also engage with state and territory governments
regarding potential for schemes established in state and territory legislation to adopt similar reforms.
The Government considers that these reforms will make an important contribution to addressing
financial abuse and ensuring the superannuation system does not facilitate ongoing financial
advantage for perpetrators. The Government will continue to engage with stakeholders, including
victim-survivor advocates, industry participants and state and territory governments, as it progresses
these reforms.

Government is committed to monitoring the effectiveness of recent family law reforms in
strengthening protections for victim-survivors and preventing the misuse of legal processes to inflict
financial harm.
The Government is required to commence a review of the Family Law Amendment Act 2023 by 6
May 2027 and the Family Law Amendment Act 2024 by 10 June 2028. These reviews will provide an
opportunity to assess the operation and effectiveness of recent reforms, including their impact on
victim-survivors experiencing financial abuse.
The Family Law Amendment Act 2023 introduced measures to reduce systems abuse within the
family law system, including by enabling courts to make harmful proceedings orders to prevent
parties from using family law proceedings to cause harm, including financial harm. The reforms also
enable courts to make costs orders where parties fail to comply with their duty to conduct
proceedings in a manner that facilitates the just, efficient and cost-effective resolution of disputes.
The Family Law Amendment Act 2024 strengthened the recognition of financial abuse in family law
property proceedings, including by clarifying the matters courts may consider when determining
property settlements.
The Government will continue to monitor the implementation and effectiveness of these reforms,
informed by engagement with courts, legal assistance services, family violence experts and people
with lived experience, to ensure the family law system appropriately recognises and responds to
financial abuse.

specialised training in domestic and family violence, including financial abuse.
Australian Government Response
The Government support this recommendation in principle.
The Government recognises the importance of ensuring that people experiencing family and
domestic violence, including financial abuse, can access timely and appropriately tailored support
when engaging with government services. Dedicated teams with specialised expertise can play an

A number of Commonwealth agencies already have dedicated frontline service teams with
specialised expertise in family and domestic violence, including financial abuse. For example, Services

and domestic violence through a national network of service centres, telephony services, online

The ATO also has specialist teams that provide support to people experiencing, or at risk of

The Government acknowledges the value of specialist expertise in supporting victim-survivors and

The Government will continue to work with Commonwealth agencies to strengthen capability,
improve frontline responses and ensure service delivery arrangements appropriately support people
experiencing family and domestic violence, including financial abuse, as part of its broader efforts to
embed safety across government systems and services.

Emerging issues and areas for ongoing
consideration
The Government recognises that the Committee’s report made an important contribution to
understanding the prevalence, impacts and drivers of financial abuse in Australia and provided a
comprehensive framework for strengthening protections for victim-survivors. The Government also
acknowledges that financial abuse is not static. As government systems, financial products and
service delivery models evolve, new risks can emerge and existing forms of abuse can manifest in
different ways. The Government therefore considers it important that efforts to address financial
abuse extend beyond the specific recommendations contained in the report and remain responsive
to emerging evidence, stakeholder engagement and lived experience.
Since the Committee tabled its report, the Government has continued to engage with victim-
survivors, advocacy organisations, regulators, industry participants and the Parliament through its
broader work on financial abuse, systems abuse and family and domestic violence. Through these
engagements, several additional issues have been identified which, while not specifically addressed
by the Committee’s recommendations, raise similar concerns regarding the misuse of legal, financial
and administrative systems to exercise control, undermine autonomy, create economic harm or
exacerbate vulnerability.
These issues highlight the broad range of settings in which financial abuse can occur and reinforce
the importance of applying the principles underpinning this Government Response across a wider
ecosystem of institutions and systems. Common themes include the potential misuse of positions of
authority, information asymmetries, barriers to informed decision-making, inadequate recognition of
vulnerability, and the need for systems and services to be designed and operated in ways that
promote safety, dignity and economic security
The Government considers the following matters to be areas warranting ongoing attention and
engagement as part of the broader body of work focused on financial abuse, coercive control,
systems abuse and economic wellbeing.
Public trustees, guardianship arrangements and financial autonomy
The Government recognises stakeholder concerns regarding the operation of public trustee and
substitute decision-making arrangements, particularly in circumstances where individuals may have
limited capacity to independently manage their financial affairs. Stakeholders have noted that public
trustees can exercise significant authority over a person's financial and property interests, including
decisions relating to investments, property transactions, contractual arrangements and other
financial matters.
Recent stakeholder advocacy has highlighted concerns regarding the adequacy of safeguards that
apply to public trustee arrangements and whether existing mechanisms consistently support the
rights, preferences and autonomy of individuals whose affairs are managed by a trustee or guardian.
Concerns raised by stakeholders include the impact of trustee fees and charges on long-term
financial wellbeing, questions regarding consultation and participation in decision-making, and the
broader management of financial affairs on behalf of vulnerable individuals

legislation. In June 2026, Senator Lidia Thorpe proposed amendments to the Treasury Laws
Amendment (Delivering an Efficient and Trusted Tax System) Bill 2026 which would have inserted

Financial abuse within corporate, insolvency and external administration
systems
The Government also notes emerging stakeholder concerns regarding the intersection between
financial abuse and Australia’s corporate, insolvency and external administration frameworks.
Evidence gathered through broader engagement on financial abuse has highlighted that corporate
structures, business arrangements and insolvency processes may, in some circumstances, be used to
facilitate or perpetuate coercive control and financial harm.
The Government recognises that insolvency practitioners, corporate regulators and other
participants in the corporate system may encounter circumstances involving family and domestic
violence, financial abuse and coerced participation in corporate activities. These issues may arise in
the context of company management, business ownership arrangements, corporate liabilities,
insolvency proceedings or the misuse of legal and regulatory processes.
Stakeholder engagement has highlighted the importance of ensuring that professionals operating
within the corporate and insolvency system are equipped to identify indicators of financial abuse and
understand the ways in which perpetrators may utilise business and corporate structures to cause
economic harm. The Government notes that ASIC has encouraged liquidators to be mindful of the
signs of family and domestic violence issues within the insolvency profession and continues to
engage in broader policy discussions relating to coerced directorships and financial abuse.
These issues complement the Government’s existing work to address coerced directorships and the
misuse of tax and corporate systems to create debts and liabilities as a form of coercive control. They

Insurance responses to trauma associated with domestic, family and sexual
violence
The Government further notes concerns raised by victim-survivor advocates regarding the treatment
of trauma arising from domestic, family and sexual violence within insurance and superannuation
insurance settings. Stakeholders have argued that existing underwriting, risk assessment and claims
management practices may not always appropriately recognise the unique circumstances of victim-
survivors who experience psychological injury or trauma as a consequence of violence perpetrated
against them.
In particular, stakeholders have raised concerns that trauma-related mental health conditions arising
from domestic, family and sexual violence may, in some cases, be considered as pre-existing
conditions for the purposes of insurance coverage decisions. Stakeholders contend that such
approaches can affect access to insurance products or entitlements and may create additional
barriers for victim-survivors seeking financial security and recovery following experiences of violence.
These concerns have been raised across a range of insurance settings, including income protection,
total and permanent disability insurance and workers’ compensation arrangements.
The Government recognises that victim-survivors should not be placed in a position where seeking
support or treatment for trauma creates concerns regarding future access to insurance products or
services. The Government also acknowledges stakeholder concerns that insurance settings which are
perceived as punitive or discriminatory may discourage disclosure, treatment-seeking or engagement
with support services.
Stakeholders have proposed a range of responses, including greater adoption of trauma-informed
approaches, enhanced workforce training, consideration of anti-discrimination principles and closer
collaboration between insurers and victim-survivor advocacy organisations. Industry participants and
advocacy organisations have also commenced broader discussions regarding how insurance
products, underwriting frameworks and claims processes can better reflect the realities of trauma
and recovery.
These issues are closely aligned with the Committee’s recommendations concerning the role of the
insurance sector in preventing and responding to financial abuse. They also reinforce the importance
of ensuring that financial products and services support, rather than undermine, the recovery,
independence and long-term economic security of victim-survivors.

Continuing engagement and future
reform
The Committee's report has reinforced the importance of addressing financial abuse as a whole-of-
system issue. The recommendations contained in this report, together with the emerging issues
identified since the report was tabled, demonstrate that financial abuse can occur across a broad
range of financial, legal and administrative settings and is often interconnected with family and
domestic violence, elder abuse, disability-related exploitation, systems abuse and other forms of
coercive control.
The Government's response to the Committee's recommendations forms part of a broader program
of work to strengthen protections against financial abuse, embed safety by design across
government systems and services, and improve outcomes for victim-survivors. This work includes
implementing the Government's commitments arising from the Commonwealth Systems Abuse
Audit, progressing reforms to prevent the misuse of tax and corporate systems as tools of coercive
control, strengthening responses to financial abuse within child support and taxation systems, and
advancing the objectives of the National Plan to End Violence Against Women and Children 2022-
2032 and Working for Women: A National Strategy for Gender Equality.
The Government recognises that the issues examined by the Committee do not exist in isolation.
Emerging concerns relating to public trustee arrangements, substitute decision-making frameworks,
corporate and insolvency processes, and insurance practices highlight that many of the same
underlying risks identified by the Committee including misuse of authority, barriers to financial
autonomy, inadequate safeguards for vulnerable individuals and opportunities for systems to be
weaponised can arise across a range of settings.
Accordingly, the Government will continue to consider these issues alongside implementation of the
Committee's recommendations and broader reforms to address financial abuse and systems abuse.
The Government will work across portfolios, regulators and sectors to identify common themes,
share lessons from reform initiatives and ensure that responses remain informed by emerging
evidence, stakeholder perspectives and lived experience.
The Government recognises that preventing financial abuse requires more than reforming individual
programs or institutions. It requires ongoing efforts to ensure that financial, legal and administrative
systems are designed and operated in ways that support safety, autonomy, informed decision-
making and economic security. The Government will continue to engage with victim-survivors,
advocacy organisations, industry, regulators, academics, state and territory governments and other
stakeholders to identify opportunities to strengthen protections, improve accountability and reduce
opportunities for exploitation across systems.
Through implementation of this response, and through its broader work to prevent family and
domestic violence and systems abuse, the Government will continue to pursue a coordinated and
evidence-based approach that recognises the interconnected nature of these issues and supports the
safety, dignity, autonomy and financial wellbeing of all Australians.
