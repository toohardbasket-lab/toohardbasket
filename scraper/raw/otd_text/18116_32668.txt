Australian Government response to the Senate Economics Legislation Committee Report
Introduction
The Treasury Laws Amendment (Financial Reporting System Reform) Bill 2026 (the Bill) amends
Part 12 ‘The Australian financial reporting system’ of the Australian Securities and Investments
Commission Act 2001 (ASIC Act) to establish External Reporting Australia (ERA) to replace the
Financial Reporting Council (FRC), the Australian Accounting Standards Board (AASB), the
Auditing and Assurance Standards Board (AUASB) and the Offices of the AASB and AUASB.
Following introduction of the Bill into Parliament on 12 February 2026, the Bill was referred to the
Senate Economics Legislation Committee (Committee) for inquiry and report. The Committee’s
report on the Bill was released on 24 April 2026.
The Committee’s report recommended that the Bill be passed. The Government’s response to the
recommendations contained in the Coalition Senators’ Dissenting Report and the Australian Greens’
Additional Comments is set out herein.
The Bill implements the Government’s announcement of 21 November 2023 to merge the FRC, AASB
and AUASB into a single flexible entity. The proposal was discussed in the December 2022 Treasury
consultation paper, Climate-related financial disclosure.
The new entity, ERA, will have the combined key functions and powers of the AASB, the AUASB
(and their Offices) and the FRC. ERA will be responsible for making and formulating accounting,
auditing and assurance, and sustainability standards.
The Bill was passed by the Parliament, with amendments, on 30 June 2026 and received Royal Assent
on 6 July 2026.

Response to the recommendations
Coalition Senators’ Dissenting Report
Recommendation 1
That the Bill not be passed in its current form.
Australian Government Response
The Bill was passed by the Parliament on 30 June 2026.
Recommendation 2
That Treasury conduct and publish a cost-benefit analysis of the changes before further scrutiny by
the Senate.
Australian Government Response
The Government does not support this recommendation. The Bill was passed by the Parliament.
The introduction of the Bill followed multiple rounds of public consultation with stakeholders and
consideration of stakeholder feedback, including:
• In December 2022, where feedback was sought on various structural options for the setting of
standards to support the introduction of climate-related financial disclosure reporting
requirements
• In January 2025, where the Government released a consultation paper outlining a proposed
model for the new combined body
• In October to November 2025, with the release of exposure draft legislation and explanatory
materials.
Stakeholder feedback was broadly supportive of the reform. The explanatory memorandum to the
Bill sets out the Government’s rationale for the reform.

Recommendation 3
Preserve parliamentary scrutiny for all standards by making them disallowable instruments.
Australian Government Response
The Government does not support this recommendation but notes that under the Bill, a standard
will be disallowable in accordance with its status as a legislative instrument at the time it was made,
consistent with the legislative character test in subsection 8(4) of the Legislation Act 2003. The Bill
was passed by the Parliament without amendments for this recommendation.
As set out in the explanatory memorandum to the Bill, the Bill seeks to apply the legislative character
test in subsection 8(4) of the Legislation Act 2003, while recognising that as a matter of practicality
the status of standards should be fixed at the time the instrument is made.
A standard is a legislative instrument if, at the time it is formulated, any part of the standard could
reasonably be expected to affect a person’s rights or obligations under a law of the Commonwealth,
whether directly or indirectly.
Standards other than for the purposes of the corporations legislation do not have legal effect under
the Corporations Act 2001 or the ASIC Act and would generally be non-binding. However, at the time
they are formulated, some standards may have immediate legal effect under other Commonwealth
laws that automatically incorporate standards made by ERA. This could be the case where, when
read with those other laws, the standard would impose obligations established by those laws. Such
standards could reasonably be expected to affect a person’s rights or obligations and hence are to be
made by legislative instrument. Where these considerations are not applicable, ERA has the flexibility
to make the standard by non-legislative instrument, which may be considered a more accurate
reflection of the administrative character of a non-binding standard.
Recommendation 4
Require an independent post-implementation review after 2 years, to be tabled in Parliament.
Australian Government Response
The Government does not support this recommendation. The Bill was passed by the Parliament

The ERA will be a key institution within Australia’s economic infrastructure. As part of the

As the accountable authority, ERA’s Governing Council will be responsible for discharging its
obligations under the Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013, which includes
ensuring the proper use and management of its resources, assessing its performance, reporting on its

Recommendation 5
Prohibit the Governing Council from making or amending technical standards itself to maintain the
independence of technical standard setters.
Australian Government Response
The Government does not support this recommendation. The Bill was passed by the Parliament
without amendment for this recommendation.
As set out in the explanatory memorandum to the Bill, the Bill reflects a balance in approach
between two key objectives:
1. Ensuring that the detailed work of day-to-day standard setting is primarily carried out by
specialist boards with appropriate technical expertise supported by experienced staff.
2. Ensuring the Governing Council maintains the capacity to discharge its primary role as ERA’s
accountable authority under the Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013
framework in recognition of the responsibilities it has for the oversight and ultimate
performance of all of ERA’s functions.
Under the Bill, special procedural requirements apply before the Governing Council makes,
formulates, varies, revokes or gives a direction in relation to a particular standard that is the
responsibility of a standard-setting board. This provides a higher threshold for the Governing Council
to intervene in the more detailed aspects of standard setting in recognition that standard-setting
boards are to be established to primarily carry out these functions for ERA.
The explanatory memorandum clarifies that, where the Governing Council is concerned about a
standard-setting board’s work in making, formulating, varying, or revoking a particular standard, and
its concern has not or cannot be addressed through internal engagement with the relevant board, it
is expected that the Governing Council would preference providing a written direction to the board
to address the issue. It would likely only be in exceptional or urgent circumstances where the
Governing Council may find it necessary to take direct action by seeking to issue, amend or revoke a
standard itself to address concerns that have arisen.

Recommendation 6
Remove clauses 225A(2) and 225B from the Bill and define functions in legislation.
Australian Government Response
The Government does not support this recommendation. The Bill was passed by the Parliament
without amendment for this recommendation.
Subsection 225A(2) provides the Minister may by legislative instrument specify new functions and
procedural requirements in relation to those new functions. Empowering the Minister to specify
additional functions ensures ERA can achieve a key objective of the reforms, being to add flexibility
to the institutional arrangements and better position the body to respond to future needs relating to
standard setting. Amendment to the Corporations Act 2001 (or other laws) would be required to
make compliance with any new types of standards mandatory.
Section 225B provides that ERA may make standards other than for the purposes of the
corporations legislation by legislative instrument or by non-legislative instrument. As conveyed to the
Senate Standing Committee for the Scrutiny of Bills (Scrutiny Digest 5 of 2026 (25/03/2026) refers),
the test in section 225B is intended to involve a factual enquiry about whether the standard could
affect a person’s rights or obligations under a law of the Commonwealth at the time it is made. ERA
would not be responsible or answerable for any subsequent incorporation, adoption or application of
a standard, even in circumstances where ERA intends that a non-legislative standard would or may
later be incorporated under other Commonwealth legislation. Instead, it is appropriate for
Parliament to scrutinise any incorporation by reference at a later time, such as by disallowing the
instrument that seeks to apply the non-legislative standard.
Recommendation 7
Require publication of all decisions.
Australian Government Response
The Government does not support this recommendation. The Bill was passed by the Parliament
without amendment for this recommendation.
The transparency mechanisms already provided for in the Bill are sufficient and appropriate. As set
out in the explanatory memorandum to the Bill, ERA will be bound by certain requirements that
support transparency. These include:
• The requirements that certain actions taken by the Minister, Governing Council and
standard-setting boards be done by legislative instrument – meaning they will be
subject to appropriate consultation requirements and scrutiny by Parliament under the
Legislation Act 2003.

Australian Greens Additional Comments
Recommendation 1
That the Bill be amended to:
• Require that the Governing Council and standards-setting boards do not include individuals with a
current financial interest in entities from large accounting and auditing firms, and
• Add ‘ethics’ to the list of fields that the Minister is satisfied the person has experience in.
Australian Government Response
The Government agreed to this recommendation. The Bill was passed by the Parliament with
amendments to give effect to this recommendation.
Recommendation 2
That the Bill be amended to:
• Require that External Reporting Australia act in the public interest, and
• Retain the Financial Reporting Council’s mandatory information-gathering powers
Australian Government Response
The Government agreed in part to this recommendation. The Bill was passed by the Parliament with
amendments regarding ERA acting in the public interest.
Amendments were not adopted to retain the FRC’s mandatory information-gathering powers. These
powers are connected with the FRC’s existing function of providing strategic policy advice and
reports in relation to the quality of audits conducted by Australian auditors, which will not be a
function of ERA in its role as a standard-setting body. The explanatory memorandum notes that in
practice assessing the internal systems of auditors and their firms is already conducted by the
Australian Securities and Investments Commission which, as the regulator, has access to compulsory
powers and an investigative remit to carry out and report on this work.
