     Introduction     
  The Australian Government welcomes the opportunity to respond to     the Senate Economics Committee’s (the Committee) report         Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC) investigation and enforcement    .   

                


   Response to the recommendations                                     


        Recommendation 1   


   The committee recommends that the Australian Government should recognise that the Australian Securities and Investments Commission has comprehensively failed to fulfil its regulatory remit.                     


       Recommendation 2   


   The committee recommends that the Australian Government should recognise, based on the finding of recommendation one, that the Australian Securities and Investments Commission’s regulatory failures call into question whether its remit is too broad for it to be an effective and efficient agency, and the government should strongly consider separating its functions between a     companies     regulator and a separate financial conduct authority.   


       Australian Government response    


      t    he Government         do        es         not support         recommendation    s     1 and 2    .   


   The     Government     acknowledges that ASIC has a wide regulatory remit but     is confident in ASIC’s ability to fulfil its responsibilities     and will continue to take up opportunities to enhance its effectiveness    .   


   The Government is committed to supporting ASIC to fulfil its responsibilities         through appropriate resourcing, strong governance, and effective leadership.     The Government         increased ASIC’s     budget     from $    435.0     million in     2022-23     to $    676.8     million in the     2026-27     Budget    .    


   The Government     appointed three new Commissioners in 2023,     Ms     Simone Constant,     Mr         Alan         Kirkland     and         Ms     Kate O’Rourke    .     The Government also announced the appointment of     Ms         Sarah Court as the new ASIC Chair,     who commenced     on 1 June 2026    .    


   The Government remains committed to ensuring Australians have confidence in the financial system and the necessary protections from financial harm         and will continue to identify opportunities to improve consumer outcomes and enhance the performance of Australia’s financial system    .       
                                         


      Recommendation     3   

       The committee recommends that the Australian Government urgently address the shortcomings in Australia’s system for handling reports of alleged corporate misconduct. In doing so, the committee recommends that the Australian Government make it a legislative requirement of the Australian Securities and Investments Commission or future regulatory authorities to investigate reports of alleged misconduct at an appropriate rate. Further, the committee recommends that:   


   the regulator develop consistent standards to transparently report data to the public on the handling of reports of alleged misconduct; and   


   the regulator     establish     service standards to require that people who submit reports of alleged misconduct are provided with clear, detailed and timely information on the tangible actions taken in response to their report.   


        Australian Government response    


       t    he Gov    ernment         do        es         not support         this         recommendation    .   

    ASIC’s approach to handling reports of alleged corporate misconduct is critical to ensuring Australians have confidence in the financial system.   

    In accordance with ASIC’s enforcement approach,     ASIC consider    s     information from a range of sources when selecting matters for formal investigation    , including     reports of     alleged     misconduct.        

    Mandating investigations at a prescribed rate     would undermine     ASIC’s     regulatory discretion and ability to prioritise matters posing the greatest risk. ASIC typically commences around 200     to 250     formal investigations and between 100 and 150 court cases each year    .        

    ASIC release    s     regular public reports on the outcomes of its enforcement including in its Annual Report and bi-annual summary of enforcement outcomes.   


   In 2024-25, ASIC recorded a 50 per cent increase in investigations, and     an     almost 20         per         cent increase in new civil enforcement proceedings from the previous financial year.    


           In the second half of 2025 (July-December 2025), ASIC commenced 123 new investigations and 23 new civil proceedings, commenced 11 new criminal prosecutions and had                 17                 criminal convictions recorded against individuals in connection with ASCI investigations.   


            In the last six months, ASIC has secured civil penalties totally $349.8 million, the highest         six        -        monthly         civil penalty total in ASIC’s history and delivered $583 million back to tens of thousands of customers and investors as part of remediation, refunds, and payments in connection with ASIC’s work.    
  ASIC is accountable to     the Parliament for its enforcement and compliance activities through its Annual Report and oversight from the Parliamentary Joint Committee on Corporations and Financial Services    , Senate Standing Committee on Economics and House of Representatives Economics Committee.      

                                        


       Recommendation     4   
  The committee recommends that the statement of expectations which is currently issued for the Australian Securities and Investments Commission:   


   contain, among other things, expectations and priorities relating to transparency; and   


   be provided in draft form to the Parliamentary Joint Committee on Corporations and Financial Services for inquiry and report.        


        Australian Government response    


       t    he Government         do        es         not support     recommendation.   
     Statements of Expectations enable ministers to provide clarity on whole-of-government objectives and expectations relevant to a regulator, including the policies and priorities it is expected to observe in meeting its mandate and exercising its powers.   
     Statements of Expectations sit alongside established parliamentary scrutiny arrangements, including through Senate Estimates, committee inquiries, and statutory reporting obligations    .   
  The Government issued     a     new Statement of Expectations for     the     Australian Securities and Investments Commission on 16 July     2026    .    


         

                                       


        Recommendation     5   

    The committee recommends that the Australian Government make it a legislated regulatory objective of the Australian Securities and Investments Commission or other regulatory authorities to establish and maintain a high level of transparency of investigation and enforcement outcomes. Additionally, the committee recommends that these transparency objectives be supported by:   


    establishing a searchable public register of civil or criminal outcomes arising from reports of alleged misconduct received and the outcome of the proposed regulatory authorities’ handling those reports, subject to appropriate thresholds,     similar to     the approach taken by the US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau; and   


    developing a consistent, long-term public reporting framework that quantifies and assesses the proposed regulatory authorities’ performance and capacity to undertake its regulatory functions of investigating and enforcing breaches of     corporations     law.   


        Australian Government response    


       t    he Gov    ernment         do        es         not support     th    is     recommendation    .   

    The Government supports appropriate transparency of enforcement outcomes.   

    ASIC         already         publish    es     outcomes         of its investigative and enforcement activities     on a regular basis    , including through     its     A    nnual     R    eport and     Co    rporate     P    lan.     ASIC also     issues     timely media releases which detail the outcomes of specific enforcement activities i    t     has     undertaken     and         also     provides     consumer warnings.   

    ASIC    ’s website provides access to     registers of people and organisations         that are disqualified or banned from roles in corporations, financial services or the credit industry    . These enable the public to access a range of information including names of persons or organisations, the type of ban or disqualification and     the     date it came into effect.   

    Any enhancements to ASIC’s website such as a searchable public register are a matter for ASIC to consider within its existing resourcing.   

        ASIC is subject to a range of existing public accountability mechanisms including:   


        Reporting requirements including Annual Portfolio Budget Statements, Corporate Plans and Annual Reports   


        The     Commonwealth Ombudsman, which considers complaints about ASIC’s administrative actions, and the     Australian National Audit Office (ANAO)    ,     which conducts     annual     audits     of ASIC’s financial accounts and     ad hoc     performance audits of ASIC’s functions   


        Parliamentary oversight from the Parliamentary Joint Committee on Corporations and Financial Services    ,     Senate Standing Committee on Economics     (Economics Legislation Committee and Economics References Committee)     and     House of     Representatives Economics Committee   


        Regular     reviews by the     Financial Regulator Assessment Authority (FRAA), which is responsible for conducting independent reviews of ASIC’s effectiveness and capability    .       

                                    


      Recommendation     6   
   The committee recommends that the Australian Government investigate amending the whistleblower protection provisions in the         Corporations Act 2001     to include pecuniary incentives and compensation for whistleblowers who make a substantiated disclosure. The committee recommends that the pecuniary provisions be examined with a view to:   


   establishing a financial incentive for whistleblowers to make a disclosure in circumstances where addressing the misconduct would result in a significant public benefit; and   


   establishing a financial compensation mechanism for whistleblowers who are unable to make a disclosure in the public benefit without experiencing significant personal detriment, such as loss of career prospects.    


       Australian Government response    


      t    he Government             notes     this     recommendation.   
  T    he Treasury is currently conducting a statutory review into Australia’s tax and corporate whistleblowing frameworks. The review will investigate whether the current laws are working as intended, identify any ongoing concerns, and where appropriate make recommendations for further improvement.    
  The     r    eview follows amendments in 2019 which established a new tax whistleblowing regime in the        Taxation Administration Act 1953     and strengthened     the     corporate and financial services sector whistleblowing regimes in the        Corporations Act 2001    .    
  As part of the statutory review, Treasury will holistically consider whether     existing     protection    s    , assistance and support mechanisms for whistleblowers are effective. This     will include     consideration of the merits of financial incentives for whistleblowers in the Australian context    .                    


          Recommendation 7   

         The committee recommends that regulatory authorities adopt an enforcement approach which prioritises the litigation of all serious instances of suspected breaches of        corporations        law, particularly in cases where consumer losses arise, or could have potentially arisen, from such breaches.   


           Australian Government response    


          t       he        Government          notes        this        recommendation.   


         As an independent Commonwealth entity, ASIC is responsible for determining whether pursuing criminal proceedings, civil proceedings and/or administrative action is the most appropriate response.   


         ASIC consider       s        a range of factors when considering which enforcement action to pursue               as part of a targeted, risk-based and proportionate approach,        including:   


         Nature and seriousness of the        suspected misconduct   


         Conduct of the person or entity after the suspected misconduct   


         Strength of evidence   


         Expected public benefit in taking enforcement action   


         Likelihood of behavioural improvement and deterrence   


         Mitigating factors.   


             Whilst ASIC is responsible for determining which enforcement approach to pursue, the decision to initiate and conduct criminal prosecutions is a decision of the Commonwealth Director of Public Prosecutions (CDPP). The Prosecution Policy of the Commonwealth provides guidelines on decision-making in the initiation and conduct of prosecutions.       

     
                                      


           Recommendation     8   

    The committee recommends that the Australian Government review a new governance structure for the Australian Securities and Investments Commission or any new regulatory bodies. This structure would have a Chair or Chief Executive Officer as sole statutory appointee and accountable     authority     and the appropriateness of the commission structure entirely should be explored.                    


        Recommendation     9   

    The committee recommends that the Australian Government should ensure that a legislated code of conduct be included as part of the governing documents of ASIC or any alternative regulatory bodies, and that the Chair and any other statutory appointees can be sanctioned for workplace misconduct that is found to have breached this code. Further, the committee recommends that the Australian Government establish a mechanism by which an alleged breach of this code of conduct by a statutory appointee can be examined by an appropriately independent and qualified panel.   


        Australian Government response    


       t    he     Government         do        es         not support     recommendation    s     8 and 9    .   

    ASIC’s governance and accountability     arrangements a    re consistent with     other comparable regulators     that are also     independent Commonwealth     entit    ies    .         The    se arrangements     reflect the legislative framework within which ASIC operates.   

    In accordance with     the         Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013             (PGPA Act)    ,     the ASIC Chair governs ASIC and has sole executive management responsibility.     ASIC’s Chair         is the Accountable Authority under section 9A of the         Australian Securities and Investments Commission Act 2001     (ASIC Act)    .   

    The Commission acts as a strategic non-executive body, focusing on high-level regulatory and statutory decision making and stakeholder management. It provides support to the Chair on organisational oversight. Several committees assist with the effective and efficient performance of the Commission’s regulatory and governance roles.   

    ASIC’s Chair and Commissioners are accountable under the     PGPA Act    .     ASIC’s Chair and Commissioners must     perform their functions and exercise their duties with the degree of care and diligence that a reasonable person would in the same circumstances    .   

    ASIC Commissioners are also accountable to ASIC’s Code of Conduct (the Code)    , which was     released in August 2023.     This Code     applie    s     to     all     ASIC     Commissioners,     employees, contractors, consultants, secondees and volunteers    .         If ASIC finds that a     person     has breached the Code, they may be subject to disciplinary action    , for example,     termination of employment.                 


      Recommendation     10   
  The committee recommends that the Australian Government reverse its decision, announced in the 2023–24 Budget, to reduce the frequency of Financial Regulator Assessment Authority (FRAA) reviews from every two years to every five years. Further, the committee recommends that the FRAA undertake an inquiry into the effectiveness of the oversight mechanisms of corporate regulators.   


       Australian Government response    


      t    he Gov    ernment         do        es         not support     this recommendation    .   
  The Treasury Laws Amendment (Strengthening Financial Systems and Other Measures) Bill 2025 (the Bill) received Royal Assent on 4 December 2025. Schedule 3 to the Bill amended the FRAA Act to reduce the frequency of FRAA’s reviews of ASIC and     Australian Prudential Regulation Authority         (    APRA    )     to every five years. This change allows     for time between reviews for the regulators to address prior recommendations    .   
  The FRAA was established     in response     to     Recommendation 6.13 and 6.14 of the     Financial Services Royal Commission         which specified the authority focus on     APRA and ASIC    .                       


      Recommendation     11   
  The committee recommends that the Australian Government reassess the funding arrangements for the Australian Securities and Investments Commission or any alternative regulatory authority so that:   


   a greater level of funding can be directly resourced with the proceeds of regulatory fines—including late fees, court fines, penalties and infringement notices;   


   all reasonable steps are taken to ensure levies charged on industry subsectors under the Industry Funding Model are reduced commensurate with increased resourcing to the regulator through the proceeds of fines; and   


   it is ensured that regulatory authorities are accountable for the level of resourcing linked to cost-recovered activity, and face obligations to rationalise surplus resourcing to reduce costs on the industry subsector participants.           


       Australian Government response    


      t    he     Government         do        es         not         support     this recommendation    .   
  ASIC’s funding is determined by the Government through the annual Budget process.     The Government increased ASIC’s budget from $435.0 million in 2022-23 to $676.8 million in the 2026-27 Budget.   
  A proportion of this funding is recovered from industry     through the     Industry Funding Model (IFM). The IFM is an     'industry pays' model     whereby     the funding of regulatory activities undertaken by ASIC is met by those creating the need for regulation, rather than the Australian taxpayer.   
   C    onsistent with the     Australian     Constitution    ,     government policy and legislative frameworks     (    including the         Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Rule 2014    ),             regulatory fines         —         including late fees, court fines, penalties and infringement notices         recovered by ASIC         —         are required to be paid into the     Consolidated Revenue Fund     and cannot be used to fund ASIC’s functions.     The purpose of these amounts being directed to     the     Consolidated Revenue Fund         is to ensure that     enforcement decisions are driven by statutory         objectives and the public interest, not by revenue     raising considerations    .     
  ASIC’s     IFM     is transparent and consistent with Commonwealth legislative requirements and the     Australian Government Charging     Framework. ASIC publish    es     a range of public documents which detail how ASIC     apportion    s         its         regulatory costs in the financial year for each sector, the type of activity undertaken and the different kinds of expenses incurred.
